# B2B Reporting Style — Toggle

Used for Atlassian Marketplace app partners (B2B SaaS companies selling Jira/Confluence plugins). May expand to other B2B industries in future.

---

## Growth partner mindset for B2B reports

B2B clients — especially Atlassian Marketplace partners — often have technical product teams but lean marketing functions. Toggle's value is not just running ads efficiently but helping the client understand what the ad data reveals about their product, positioning, and funnel. This means:

- When ads are driving clicks but not conversions, ask whether the problem is the ad or the Marketplace listing — and say so
- When certain keywords convert well, they often reveal which pain points resonate most with users — share that insight, as it has implications beyond ads (product messaging, website copy, content strategy)
- When a campaign is limited by budget but performing well, make the case for scaling — don't just note it
- When non-converting keywords point to a mismatch between what users are searching for and what the product does, flag the positioning gap
- When the US dominates conversions and other markets underperform, help the client think about whether their go-to-market focus should shift, not just whether to exclude those countries from targeting

One or two of these strategic observations per report is sufficient. They should be grounded in the data and framed constructively — not as criticism but as opportunities.

Common growth observations for Atlassian Marketplace clients:
- **Marketplace listing quality:** If CTR is strong but conversion rate is low, the drop-off is likely at the listing level — app screenshots, description, reviews, or pricing may need attention
- **Keyword intent signals:** High-converting keywords reveal real user pain points — these are valuable inputs for product positioning, feature prioritisation, and content marketing
- **Evaluation-to-install gap:** If Cloud Try Clicks are high but actual installs are low, there may be a product onboarding or trial experience issue worth investigating
- **Market concentration:** Heavy US dependence may indicate an opportunity to localise messaging or listing content for other English-speaking markets (UK, Australia, Canada)

## Tone and voice

- Professional, analytical, and consultative
- More detailed reasoning than B2C — commentary explains not just what happened but what it implies strategically
- Uses first-person plural: "we", "our", "we recommend", "we have since..."
- Bold text used heavily to highlight key terms, metrics, and findings within commentary
- Avoids em dashes — use connecting words instead
- Technical language is acceptable (e.g. "search intent alignment", "negative keyword exclusion", "keyword-to-intent alignment")

## Slide structure

B2B reports go deeper than B2C and typically include more breakdown slides per platform. Standard structure:

1. **Cover slide**
2. **Per platform — Campaign Overview slide** (KPI summary + Key Points)
3. **Per platform — Campaign Breakdown slide** (campaign-level table + Key Points + Next Steps)
4. **Per platform — Keyword Performance slide** (keyword table + Key Points + Next Steps)
5. *(Additional slides as needed: Ad Group Performance, Country Performance, Demand Gen, Creative Performance)*
6. **Ad Optimisations Conducted slide** (bullet list of what was done this period)
7. **Next Steps slide** (consolidated, may use Keep/Start/Stop framework)
8. **Thank You closing slide**

Not all slides are required for every report — include what is relevant to the client's campaigns.

## Cover slide

Format: `[Period] / Report: / [Client Name]`

For monthly reports: `[Month Year]`
For bi-weekly reports: `[Date range, e.g. 30th Apr – 13th May]`

Toggle logo in the footer.

## KPI overview format

Unlike B2C which uses emoji KPI cards, B2B uses the **platform's native dashboard screenshot** as the KPI display.

For Google Ads specifically:
- Show the Google Ads dashboard screenshot (Total Cost, Total Conversions, Cost per Conversion, Avg CPC, Avg CTR, Avg Conv Rate)
- % change labels from the platform UI are shown as-is
- Key Points below the screenshot explain the numbers

## Commentary format (Key Points + Next Steps)

**Key Points:**
- Lead bullet with **bolded key terms** to draw the eye to the most important findings
  - Sub-bullet explains the implication or reasoning
    - Third-level sub-bullet for additional detail if needed
- Reference benchmarks explicitly when contextualising performance
  - "For reference, the average CPA for 2026 is €X, while the average conversion rate is X%."
  - "Compared to our past experience of running [platform] for [client], the campaigns are currently maintaining within benchmark levels."

**Next Steps:**
- More detailed than B2C — each item should explain *why* it's being done, not just *what*
- May be presented as a numbered list on the slide for clarity
- May use a **Keep / Start / Stop** framework for monthly reports (three-column table):
  - **Keep** — what's working and should be maintained
  - **Start** — new actions to begin
  - **Stop** — what should be paused or discontinued

Rules:
- Bold key phrases within bullets — metric names, campaign names, keyword names, strategic conclusions
- Always explain the reasoning behind observations (e.g. "this suggests users are in an exploratory stage rather than ready to evaluate")
- When referencing underperformance, explain the likely cause and proposed fix in the same bullet
- Avoid vague items — every next step should be specific and actionable
- Where the data points to something beyond the ad account (Marketplace listing, product onboarding, content strategy, positioning), include it as a next step or observation — framed as a growth partner recommendation, not just an ad optimisation

## Conversion types for Atlassian clients

Atlassian Marketplace app conversions are not purchases — they are app evaluation actions. Always use the correct conversion type labels:

- **Cloud Try Click** — user clicks "Try it free" on the Marketplace listing (most common)
- **Cloud Install Click** — user clicks to install the app
- **Cloud Get it Now Click** — user clicks "Get it now"

When reporting conversions, break them down by campaign and by conversion type.

## Campaign and keyword analysis slides

B2B reports include more granular breakdowns than B2C:

### Campaign Performance
- Table columns: Campaign, Cost, Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR, Avg CPC, Conversions, Cost per Conversion, Conv Rate
- Colour-coded cells from the Google Ads UI (pink for high volume metrics, green for conversions, etc.) — replicate this visual if building slides
- Key Points: identify the lead campaign driver, explain why it outperforms, flag any budget limitations or issues

### Keyword Performance
- Table: Keyword, Cost, Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR, Avg CPC, Conversions, Cost per Conversion, Conv Rate
- Show top 10 by spend or by conversions
- Key Points: name the strongest quality keyword, name any paused keywords and explain *why* they were paused, flag zero-conversion keywords with high spend

### Ad Group Performance
- Table: Ad Group, Campaign, Cost, Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR, Avg CPC, Conversions, Cost per Conversion, Conv Rate
- Key Points: compare ad group performance against account average benchmarks

### Country Performance (where applicable)
- Table: Country, Cost, Clicks, CTR, Avg CPC, Conversions, Cost per Conversion
- Key Points: identify the dominant market, flag markets with high spend and zero conversions

## Ad Optimisations Conducted slide

A dedicated slide listing all optimisations done during the reporting period. Format:

**[Platform]:**
- Action taken (e.g. "Paused low Search Impression Share keywords")
- Action taken
- New keywords added:
  - Campaign name
    - "keyword 1"
    - "keyword 2"

This slide provides accountability and shows proactive management to the client.

---

## Reporting period

- Monthly reports: full calendar month
- Bi-weekly reports: exact date range (e.g. "30th Apr – 13th May")
- Always state in Key Points if data is partial (e.g. "based on the past two weeks of data...")

---

## Currency

- Euro: **€** prefix (e.g. €18.90, €642.59)
- Note: some Atlassian clients may be billed in USD or GBP — use whatever currency the client's ad account uses

## Key differences from B2C to remember

| Dimension | B2C | B2B |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Conversational | Professional and analytical |
| Commentary length | Short and punchy | Detailed with reasoning |
| Bold text | Minimal | Heavy — key terms bolded |
| KPI display | Emoji KPI cards | Platform dashboard screenshot |
| Benchmarks | Rarely referenced | Always referenced |
| Creative analysis | Yes — thumbnails + hook/hold rates | Rarely (Google Search has no creatives) |
| Next Steps format | Agency vs client split | Numbered list or Keep/Start/Stop table |
| Conversion types | Leads, messages, LPV, purchases | Cloud Try/Install/Get it Now Clicks |
| Currency | RM | EUR (typically) |
| Report length | Shorter | Longer with more breakdown slides |
