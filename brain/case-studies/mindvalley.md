# Mindvalley Labs

_Education technology · Global (North America, Europe, Asia)_

**From $100 Leads to $10: Scaling a Global Edtech Platform Across Five Channels**

## Overview

Mindvalley is a personal growth education platform selling online courses and a subscription app to a worldwide audience. The account ran from September 2019 to March 2022 and covered three revenue lines at once: one-off product launches, a recurring membership, and a mobile app. Media ran on Google, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok and programmatic, and Toggle held channel ownership on Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok.

## Challenge

One product sold into several countries at the same time, with two revenue models running side by side. A course purchase and a subscription have different acceptable acquisition costs, so a single blended target would have hidden which half of the business was losing money. The B2B side of the account was acquiring leads at over $100 each, which made the channel unusable at any real volume. Creative was produced by four separate international agencies, with no shared standard for what counted as a winning concept.

## Solution

**1. Qualification moved into the form.** B2B acquisition shifted onto lead generation forms on LinkedIn and Facebook, with screening questions inside the form rather than after the click. This cut wasted volume before it reached the sales team.

**2. Structured creative testing across five angles.** Every round tested the same five concepts against one audience: social proof, value, content, community and benefit. Each was scored on click-through rate, install rate and cost per install, and the read-out split results by gender and age band. The community angle drew 89% male clicks and skewed 18 to 34; the benefit angle split evenly from 25 up. Two winning angles, two audiences, two budgets.

**3. Agencies briefed on variables, not taste.** Each test produced named variables for the next round, for example change this headline, swap this imagery, use this device screenshot. The four studios received instructions rather than opinions, which made their output comparable.

**4. Catalog and feed hygiene.** Product catalogs were repaired so dynamic ads could run against prospecting audiences, and five first-round beta tests were run on the Facebook platform to stay ahead of placement changes.

**Services delivered:** Performance Marketing · Creative Production · Conversion Optimisation · Agency Management

## Results

| Metric | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Cumulative media managed | $500,000 |
| Revenue from product launches | $2,000,000+ |
| Return on ad spend | 4x |
| B2B cost per lead | $100+ → $10 (90% reduction) |
| Net revenue, subscription and app | +100% |
| Cost per install | -90% |
| International creative agencies directed | 4 |
| Period | September 2019 – March 2022 |

## Key insight

The 90% cost per lead reduction came from qualifying inside the ad unit and letting a scored creative test pick the angle, not from finding cheaper inventory. Running five angles against one audience produces two winners rather than one, because different angles recruit different people. Splitting the read-out by who responded, instead of only by what performed, is what turns a creative test into an audience strategy.
