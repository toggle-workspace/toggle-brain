# B2C Reporting Style — Toggle

Used for consumer-facing clients across industries: real estate, schools, F&B, healthcare, e-commerce, veterinary, etc.

---

## Growth partner mindset for B2C reports

Toggle's role is not just to report on ad performance — it is to act as the client's growth partner. B2C clients often have limited marketing resources and are relying on Toggle to spot opportunities and risks they may not see themselves. This means:

- When a landing page is consistently underconverting despite healthy CTR, flag it — don't just recommend a creative refresh
- When a particular audience or creative angle is outperforming, consider whether it has applications beyond the current campaign (e.g. organic content, email, offers)
- When performance drops, look at external factors too — seasonality, competitor activity, product issues, or changes on the client's end
- When results are strong, help the client understand *why* so they can apply the learning elsewhere in their business

One or two of these observations per report is enough. They should feel natural, not forced. Surface them in the relevant platform's Next Steps, or bring them into the consolidated Next Steps slide if they are cross-platform in nature.

Common growth observations by B2C industry:
- **Real estate / education / healthcare (lead gen):** Lead volume vs lead quality tension — if CPL is low but conversion to actual appointments or sales is poor, flag the quality issue and suggest form qualification improvements or CRM follow-up processes
- **E-commerce:** ROAS declining despite efficient ads often signals a product, pricing, or checkout issue rather than an ads issue — raise it
- **F&B / local businesses:** Spikes tied to promotions or events are worth calling out and planning ahead for next month
- **Schools / education:** Enrolment cycles matter — help the client think about what campaigns should be running 2–3 months before their intake periods

---

## Tone and voice

- Clear, direct, and conversational — not overly formal
- Explains the "why" behind numbers, not just what happened
- Uses first-person plural: "we", "our", "we recommend"
- Avoids em dashes — use connecting words instead
- Avoids jargon without context

---

## Slide structure (per platform)

Each platform the client runs ads on gets its own set of slides. The order within each platform block is:

1. **Performance Overview slide** — KPI summary cards (see format below)
2. **Campaign Performance slide** — raw data table from the ad platform + Key Points + Next Steps
3. **Creative Performance slide** — ad thumbnails with stats + Key Points + Next Steps
4. *(Additional breakdown slides as needed — e.g. keyword performance for Google)*

After all platform blocks:

5. **Consolidated Next Steps slide** — split into agency actions and client actions (see format below)
6. **Thank You closing slide**

---

## Cover slide

Format: `[Month] [Year] / Monthly Report: / [Client Name]`

Include the agency logo (Toggle or Meraaki depending on the account).

---

## Performance Overview slide

Title: `[Platform] Performance Overview`

Include: `Reporting period: [Date range]`

KPI cards format — use emoji icons:
- 💰 Total Cost — value + MoM % change in brackets, green if positive, red if negative
- Primary result metric (Leads, Messaging Conversations, Landing Page Views, Purchases, etc.) — value + MoM %
- 📊 Cost per result — value + MoM %

Group cards by platform in a table/grid layout with the platform name as the header row (e.g. a blue header row for Meta, dark header for Google, red for TikTok).

---

## Commentary format (Key Points + Next Steps)

Used on every campaign and creative slide.

**Key Points:**
- Lead bullet states the main finding with the key metric embedded
  - Sub-bullet explains *why* it happened or what it indicates
  - Sub-bullet gives additional context if needed
- Next observation as a new lead bullet
  - Sub-bullet for explanation

**Next steps:**
- Specific, actionable item
- Another specific action
  - Sub-bullet if an action needs clarification

Rules:
- Always explain the *reason* behind a result, not just the result itself
- Keep lead bullets concise; use sub-bullets for reasoning
- Next Steps should be forward-looking and specific — avoid vague items like "continue to monitor"
- Occasionally embed a client-facing question in Next Steps using [Question] prefix, in a different colour if possible
- Use dash style bullets (not bullet symbols) throughout
- Where relevant, include one growth partner observation that goes beyond the ad account — flag landing page issues, suggest content angles, raise seasonal planning opportunities, or connect ad learnings to broader business decisions

---

## Creative Performance slide

Layout: ad thumbnails on the left, stats table on the right, Key Points + Next Steps below.

Stats to show per creative:
- Ad Name (linked if possible)
- Ad Spend
- Primary result (Leads, Conversations, LPV, etc.)
- Cost per result
- CTR
- For video ads: Video Plays, Avg. Play Time, Hook Rate, Hold Rate

Key Points on this slide should include:
- Which creative was the top performer and why (explain the creative hook or angle, not just the numbers)
- Any underperforming creatives and why

Next Steps on this slide:
- Creative refresh recommendations (specific post ideas or content directions if available)
- Pause recommendations for high-cost creatives

---

## Consolidated Next Steps slide

Title: `Next Steps`

Split into two sections:

**[Agency name — Toggle or Meraaki]**
List the agency's action items by platform, using nested bullets:
- Platform name (e.g. Meta, Google, TikTok)
  - Action item
  - Action item

**[Client name]**
List what the client needs to do or provide:
- Action items or requests
- Client-facing questions if any
- Any growth partner recommendations that require client input or decision (e.g. improving their landing page, planning a promotion, reviewing their lead follow-up process)

## Platform-specific notes

### Meta
- Campaigns are either Leads, Messaging, or Traffic objective
- Key metrics: Total Cost, Leads or Messaging Conversations or LPV, CPL or Cost/Message or CPLPV
- MoM % shown in brackets — green for improvement on the primary result metric, red for decline
- Note: lower cost and higher volume = improvement; higher CPL/CPA = decline (show in red)

### Google
- Key metrics: Total Cost, Conversions, CPA, CTR, CPC, Conversion Rate
- Include campaign-level and keyword-level breakdowns when running Search campaigns
- Always reference account benchmarks (e.g. average CPA range) to contextualise performance

### TikTok
- May run Video Views campaigns or GMV Max (e-commerce) campaigns
- For Video Views: key metrics are LPV, Cost per LPV, CTR, Hook Rate
- For GMV Max: key metrics are SKU Orders, Cost per Order, Gross Revenue, ROI
- Show both current month and previous month GMV data side by side when available

---

## Reporting period

- Standard: full calendar month (e.g. 1 May – 31 May 2026)
- If campaign started mid-month, note the actual run period in Key Points

## Currency
