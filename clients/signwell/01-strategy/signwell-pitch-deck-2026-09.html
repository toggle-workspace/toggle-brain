Finding 05 · Attribution

Your reported return sits between 1.04x and 1.43x

The August report added 481 sign-ups to paid search after the platforms reported 1,290. Those users all passed through accounts.google.com, and so does everyone who signs up with Google.

Platforms reported

1.04x

1,290 qualified sign-ups and $18,060 in value

After the report's recovery

1.43x

1,771 sign-ups, with 481 not assigned to any campaign

How the Google sign-in hop moves credit

  • Your homepage sign-up form sends people to Google to sign in, then back to SignWell.
  • Unless GA4 excludes accounts.google.com, the return trip becomes the source of the sign-up.
  • That happens to visitors from brand search, organic search, direct traffic and paid search alike.
  • Crediting all 481 to paid search overstates it. Splitting them by the visit before the hop gives the real number.

Week-one checks

  • We check the referral exclusion list on both GA4 data streams.
  • We run a test click that signs up with Google and check the click ID and the conversion.
  • We trace the session source before the hop for every sign-up that returned from Google.

Finding 06 · What Google thinks a sign-up is worth

Google values each qualified sign-up at $14

Every qualified sign-up in August carried the same $14 value. At your 7% qualified benchmark and $750 to $800 ACV, a qualified sign-up is worth $52 to $56.

Value sent today

$14

The same value applies to every campaign and every user.

Value at 7% × ACV

$52–56

That is about four times today's value.

Value range by segment

$10–56

The range depends on the conversion rate and ACV each channel earns.

Our changes

  • We key conversion values to your onboarding questionnaire: volume, company size and use case.
  • We import purchases into Google Ads, and bidding learns from revenue.
  • We raise return targets in step with the new values.

The result

Google stops treating a solo signer and a 20-seat home care agency as the same $14 event, and bids harder for the accounts that raise ACV.
