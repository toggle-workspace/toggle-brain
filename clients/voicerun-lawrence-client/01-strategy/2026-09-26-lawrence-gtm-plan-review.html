His plan in plain English

What Lawrence is trying to do, without the GTM vocabulary.

Our proposal fished with a wide net. It bought Google clicks from anyone searching for voice AI tools, and LinkedIn impressions for operations leaders in general, then tried to fix the page they landed on.

Lawrence's plan works more like a door-to-door campaign. He picks a named list of insurance companies that run large call centers. He finds the three or four people in each company who would sign off on a QA tool. Then he reaches those same people three ways at once: an email sequence, LinkedIn ads, and Google ads for anyone who searches. Every touch sends them to one new page built for insurance, with one offer. Sales picks it up from there.

The logic, as I read it: a six-person startup cannot outspend Vapi, Retell and ElevenLabs on "voice AI" in general. It can win a narrow corner where it has something specific to say, and VoiceScore for insurance call centers is that corner. The trade-off is volume. A narrow list gives fewer, better conversations, and it depends on the list being big enough to run ads against.

Who he is going after

  • Insurance carriers, MGAs (agencies that underwrite on an insurer's behalf), brokers, and BPOs (outsourced call centers) that serve insurance.
  • US headquarters or a large US operation, and at least 201 employees. His list searches also cover South America.
  • The trigger he looks for: the company is rolling out AI agents and cannot prove they work, is under pressure to cut QA cost, or finds its QA scores disagreeing with what supervisors see.

Who inside the company

  • Champion: the CX or contact center leader.
  • Day-to-day evaluator: the QA director and call center operations.
  • Technical check: the CIO, IT or AI lead, when integration comes up.
  • Money: finance or an executive sponsor, when the case rests on savings or risk.

The two messages

  • Proof and control of AI agent performance.
  • A more efficient QA operation that people trust.

His note on the buyer: QA leaders feel AI graders are unreliable. They want a system they understand, one that holds AI agents accountable the way they hold their human team accountable. There is tension between call center leaders and the executives pushing AI on them.

Where he backs our read

Useful in the meeting: these points are already agreed, so the conversation can start from them.

  • The destination is the problem. We showed Google rates the landing page Below average on 69 of 73 keywords. His website audit reaches the same place from the other side: no insurance page and no proof on the site.
  • Ops-NA is the right LinkedIn audience. Our audit had Directors and VPs of Operations clicking best. He marks Ops-NA as relevant and notes the media buy runs 1.5 to 2 times above LinkedIn's own forecast, which matches our $28.67 per site visit.
  • TechBuyer is weak. He notes the ad set loses 75% to 80% of viewers before the 25% mark of the video.
  • The offer has to be worth the form. We made bottom-of-funnel lead gen conditional on an offer. His plan leaves "Offer and CTA" as a decision VoiceRun still owes.
  • Tracking has to follow the lead into sales. His checklist asks for UTMs, key events, CRM source capture, lead routing and a named follow-up owner. That is our slide 14 loop.

What it changes for our paid media plan

  1. The media plan on slide 11 needs a rewrite

    The 35% developer-intent line and the 15% competitor line mostly leave this campaign. The new shape is $3,000 of VoiceScore search, $7,000 of LinkedIn against a named list, and $1,800 of other Google spend we may or may not run. At $7,000 a month, his LinkedIn budget alone is more than twice the $3,000 we planned for LinkedIn at the top of our range.

  2. He is building on campaigns we never audited

    His audit covers a "VoiceScore (Search)" campaign and an "Enterprise Executives (Search)" campaign. Neither appeared in the 15 July to 27 August export we audited, which held Bundled Tool, Code-First Builder and Campaign #1. He also found a quick win we can take on day one: in the Automated Call Scoring ad group, "automated QA scoring" matches software test-automation searches (LambdaTest, QA Wolf, Mabl), and those searches take 54% of that ad group's spend. About 29% of VoiceScore impressions come from real QA-buyer searches such as NICE CXone, Klaus, Scorebuddy, Gong and Calabrio.

  3. LinkedIn becomes an account-based account

    Results now depend on list size. LinkedIn needs at least 300 matched members before a matched audience will serve. If the list lands at 1,000 people, $4,000 a month works out to $4 per person per month, and frequency will climb fast. We should agree the fallback threshold to Ops-NA before launch rather than after.

  4. The $3,000 remarketing line has no pool yet

    Our audit found no LinkedIn Insight Tag on the site, so no website audience exists to remarket to. The pool will come from site visitors, video viewers and people who open a form. Installing the tag in week one starts that clock before the new page goes live.

  5. The tracking plan moves with the offer

    If VoiceRun picks "scored calls" over a demo, the primary conversion becomes the VoiceScore evaluation form on /voicescore/evaluation/ rather than demo_request. His KPIs also need page-view tracking on three pages and a HubSpot source field that survives from click to deal.

  6. The dashboard needs HubSpot and an account view

    Our SOW dashboard covers Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads and GA4. His KPIs need HubSpot demo requests and deals created. Because the plan is account-based, a useful dashboard also shows which listed accounts engaged, and it has to separate what outbound produced from what ads produced.

  7. Our role gets narrower and cleaner

    Kuota takes strategy, messaging, creative, copy and the page. That leaves us execution, optimization, tracking, reporting and the dashboard, which is the scope you described. Two lines in our paperwork overlap Kuota and should be cleaned up: the SOW's "Toggle owns ad copy drafting," and the proposal's creative strategy card on slide 6.

10 questions for Lawrence

Grouped by topic. Each has the reason behind it so you can explain the ask in one breath.

Paid media
1Which campaigns are ours to run: only the $10,000 Insurance and VoiceScore campaign, or also the $300 brand and $1,500 non-brand Google spend? Does Code-First Builder keep running inside that $1,500?

Why: Our proposal put 35% of media on developer intent. We need to know whether that work still exists, who optimizes it, and whether it shows up in our reporting.

Why: LinkedIn needs 300 matched members to serve, and a small list at $4,000 a month burns out on frequency. Your notes already name Ops-NA as the fallback, so let's agree the size that triggers it. South America would also need its own campaign, budget and probably a Spanish or Portuguese page. Can we add the ITC Vegas attendee list as its own audience?

3Where does the $3,000 LinkedIn remarketing audience come from in month one?

Why: The Insight Tag is not installed yet, so there is no website pool. I would start remarketing smaller, move the difference to prospecting, and raise remarketing as the pool fills. Is that acceptable, and should Lead Gen Forms be part of it?

4Can we get access to the VoiceScore Search and Enterprise Executives campaigns now, and start the keyword cleanup before the new page is ready?

Why: We never saw these two campaigns in our audit. The test-automation leak in Automated Call Scoring (54% of that ad group's spend) is a fix we can make in week one without waiting on anyone.

Landing page
5Who builds the Insurance and VoiceScore page, what is the go-live date, and does paid wait for it?

Why: Your main table lists VoiceRun for the build, and the landing page subpage lists Kuota. Our SOW has us recommending, not building. If the page slips, do we run Google to the existing /voicescore/ page in the meantime and hold LinkedIn until the page is live?

6What is the offer, a demo or a scored-calls evaluation, and when will VoiceRun decide?

Why: The offer sets our primary conversion event, the form fields, the LinkedIn campaign objective and what the page asks for. Your plan marks it "Decision needed," and tracking cannot be finished until it is made.

Tracking and dashboard
7Your plan lists Kuota under tracking. Which parts do you want Toggle to own?

Why: Our SOW has Toggle owning GTM, GA4 key events, Google and LinkedIn conversions, the Insight Tag and the UTM convention. Two owners on one tag container is how things break. My suggestion: Toggle owns tags and conversions, VoiceRun owns HubSpot routing, and Kuota signs off on the event list.

8Can we connect HubSpot to the dashboard, and how should a demo be credited when outbound and ads both touched the same person?

Why: Two of your five KPIs come from HubSpot, and cost per meeting depends on it. Our dashboard spec covers Google, LinkedIn and GA4 only. We also need one agreed rule for credit between Reply.io and paid, or the numbers will be argued every month.
