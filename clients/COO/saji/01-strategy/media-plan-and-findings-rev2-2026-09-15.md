### What the deck withholds, and why it withholds it this way

The deck presents RM145,000 and the outcomes it delivers. It never states a working media figure,
and it is built so that no reader can derive one. Three numbers would have given it away and all
three are absent:

1. **Cost per entry.** RM10 times 4,000 entries divided by the published 40% contest share is
   exactly RM100,000. The deck therefore reports against the entry target and shows no cost per
   entry anywhere. The one place the phrase survives is the argument that a QR code in an ad returns
   no cost per entry, which carries no figure.
2. **Blended CPM.** RM3.89 times the published 25.7M impressions is RM100,000. Slide 18 shows
   frequency instead.
3. **Any per-line or per-platform ringgit figure.** Allocation appears as percentages only, on
   slides 18 and 20.

**Speaker notes count as the deck.** Notes travel inside the .pptx, so if COO forwards the file the
notes go with it. Every note in the deck was rewritten against the same rule and the build checks it.

**The residual, recorded so nobody is surprised by it.** Slide 16 publishes every CPM and CPC, and
slide 20 publishes the allocation percentages. A determined reader could solve those two slides for
a media base close to RM100,000. Removing the rate card would cost the deck its strongest
credibility slide, so it stays. Anyone willing to do that algebra will simply ask for the media
breakdown, and that is a commercial conversation rather than a deck problem.

### The conversion rate, and the honest read on it

rev0 planned 4.0%, for a mechanic where one purchase qualified. rev1 planned 2.0%, deliberately not
crediting the pop-up, as a safety margin on a two-receipt mechanic with no precedent. **rev2 plans
3.6%, which is RM10 per entry on a RM40,000 contest line.**

The contest line buys 111,111 sessions at RM40,000, so:

| Conversion | Entries | Cost per entry, contest line | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0% | 2,222 | RM18.00 | The rev1 rate, set before the pop-up was credited |
| 3.0% | 3,333 | RM12.00 | The pop-up working partly |
| **3.6%** | **4,000** | **RM10.00** | **What the plan is built on** |
| 4.5% | 5,000 | RM8.00 | Upside if the counter QR converts at the till |

**The argument for 3.6%** is the two things 2026 did not have. A landing page returns a click, a
pixel and a conversion event the platforms can optimize toward, where 2026's WhatsApp receipt route
returned nothing measurable. And the pop-up counter puts both receipts in her hand at the moment she
is asked to enter, instead of one of them being days old.

**The honest read, which belongs in this document and not in the deck.** 3.6% sits at the confident
end of the credible band rather than the middle, and **there is no cushion left on entries.** rev1
carried a deliberate safety margin and rev2 spends it. The band from the 2025 data runs from 0.33%
to a 12.1% ceiling, so 3.6% is not a stretch in absolute terms, but it is an argued number rather
than a measured one and nothing in Saji's history or in the five Malaysian benchmarks tests it.

If the real rate comes in at 3.0%, entries land at 3,333 against a 4,000 target and there is no
budget headroom to buy the difference. **The mitigation is measurement speed, not money:** the page
reports cost per entry daily from day one, so we will know which row we are on inside the first two
weeks of a ten week contest, and we can move weight from video into contest before the window
closes. That is the plan if it happens, and it should be said out loud to COO now rather than in
March.

### The view definition problem

2026 reported 38.82M views against 51M impressions, a 76% view rate. No paid buy across Meta, TikTok
and YouTube reaches 76%, so that figure almost certainly includes KOL and organic views from the 50
influencers and the dance challenge. 40M paid views on TikTok alone would cost about RM124,000 at
RM0.0031 per view, which is more than this entire working media budget. The plan therefore treats
40M as campaign wide, and flags the assumption.

### The RM105,000 option that was available and not taken

For the record, since it may come back.

**RM105,000 of working media hits 15.00M paid reach exactly**, holding the contest line at RM40,000

### The non-paid remark

The forecast is **paid media only**. It excludes KOL and organic views from the 30 influencers, both
brands' owned channels, entries walked in from 320 sampling days across 120 outlets and 96 roadshow
days across 32 outlets, POSM and on pack QR codes, the 150,000 sample packs, the Sampul Raya, and
earned media (2026 got 10 publications at campaign launch and 10 more at the music video launch).
Actual outcomes should land above these numbers.

**It also excludes everything the pop-up costs.** Counter build, stock, staffing, the mystery gift
and the Golden Ticket packaging change are on-ground and client costs. Not a ringgit of the
RM145,000 campaign investment pays for them, and the deck says so on the caveats slide.

### Geo

The contest runs as **two separate campaigns**, not two ad sets, so budget cannot leak to the
cheapest inventory. KL and Selangor take 30%, the other states 70%. Inside each, a radius layer
around the 14 boutiques carries the pop-up message while the broad layer works the rest of the
state. The brand video runs national.

Inside a single campaign the algorithm spends where conversions are cheapest, which is KL, and the
outer states starve. **The 30/70 split still needs revisiting** against the real boutique list,
which we do not have.

## 4. Mechanics research

Five verified Malaysian benchmarks, checked 13 September 2026. Unchanged from rev1.

| Campaign | What they did | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| HeroMarket x Tyson x First Pride, 1 Aug to 30 Sep 2026 | Buy any two packs of either brand in a single receipt | Two brands works when both sit in the same basket |
| Guardian x BRAND'S x Christy Ng, 1 Sep to 31 Oct 2025 | Spend RM100 on BRAND'S, win a Christy Ng bag, 20 winners | The partner brand can be the prize rather than a second purchase |
| 100PLUS x SHALS, 17 Jan to 15 Apr 2026 | Limited edition baju raya by a Malaysian label, awarded to top spenders in TikTok Live | FMCG and fashion works when the baju is the reward |
| AEON Merdeka Shop and Win, 30 Jul to 31 Aug 2026 | RM80 single receipt, scan the QR at checkout, 69 prizes over four weekly draws | The QR lives at the till, and weekly draws hold a long window |
| Bulan Bintang Terrrpaling Raya Finale, live now | QR scan for a RM400,000 cash pool | Our partner already runs a bigger and simpler contest |

**The finding is the absence.** No Malaysian contest found required purchases from two separate
brands at two separate retailers for one entry. The HeroMarket lesson is that two brands work when
both sit in the same basket, and a Saji counter inside a Bulan Bintang boutique is how you put them
in the same basket without touching a word of what the client wrote.

### The pop-up

A Saji pop-up counter inside every one of Bulan Bintang's 14 boutiques, running the full 10 week
window from 1 February to 5 April 2027. Unchanged from rev1 and set out there in full. In short:

1. **Customer experience.** Nine steps across two retailers and two trips becomes six steps in one
   shop, in one visit, with both receipts printed minutes apart. The RM175 threshold survives intact.
2. **Accessible and exclusive at once.** Anyone can walk in, so nothing is gated. What is gated is
   the reward: the mystery gift, the campaign packaging and the Golden Ticket exist only in stock
   sold at the counters.
3. **Data.** Every pop-up receipt pairs RM25 of Saji with RM150 or more of apparel, which is
   first-party basket evidence for a higher income shopper that Saji does not hold today. **Claim
   M40, not T20.** A RM289 baju from a suburban boutique supports M40 and nothing in the evidence
   supports T20.

**The honest limitation.** The pop-up fixes the journey for anyone already heading to a boutique.
The Raya Household CFO, who does the grocery run first, still has to be persuaded through a boutique
door, which is why the radius layer exists.

### Novelty

Three devices, all gated to stock sold at the counters: a mystery gift above a RM50 counter spend
(our proposed threshold, the client sets the real one), a Saji x Bulan Bintang campaign pack sold
only at the pop-ups, and the Golden Ticket.

**Two production constraints on the Golden Ticket.** A ticket cannot go inside a bottle of cooking
oil, so it sits under the cap, on a neck tag or inside the outer carton, which is a packaging line
change with its own lead time and locks in mid December with the permit. And a randomized ticket is
a second game of chance alongside the weekly draw, so counsel should confirm whether one permit
under the Common Gaming Houses Act 1953 covers both.

### The prize

The prize pool is RM145,000, funded by the client, and **it is a separate RM145,000 from the
campaign investment.** The total does not move. The shape changes so the contest has a headline
prize rather than 50 identical ones.

| Tier | Count | Value each | Subtotal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly winners, 4 a week across 10 weeks | 40 | RM2,900 (RM500 cash + RM2,400 Saji products) | RM116,000 |
| Golden Ticket grand prize | 1 | RM29,000 | RM29,000 |
| | | **Total** | **RM145,000** |

The Golden Ticket is worth exactly ten weekly prizes. **The one trade is the winner count, 50 down
to 40.** If the client will not give up ten winners, the fallback is to keep all 50 and ask Bulan
Bintang to fund the Golden Ticket, which folds into the partner ask below.

Ten weekly draws survive, so no week passes without a visible winner. COO's working deck proposes
four draws across a 10 week window, which would leave six weeks with no winning moment. In 2025 the
contest ran roughly 2,000 winners against 4,179 entries and entries held up better that year than
any since, so visible winning is what keeps people entering. Confirm that 2025 figure against the
source deck before quoting it, since the PDF column alignment is loose.

**The partner ask.** A RM600 Bulan Bintang family baju set on each weekly prize would take it to
RM3,500 per winner with Saji's RM145,000 untouched. Worth asking, because the partner is in the
mechanic, the pop-up and the Golden Ticket but contributes nothing to what a winner receives.

### New in rev2: the kitchen redesign, as an idea

Deck slide 14. **Uncosted, not in the plan, and presented as a question.** A free kitchen renovation
was one of the prize ideas brainstormed with COO on 11 September, alongside gold hidden in product
and the Saji for a year framing, with Nestle's salary for a year campaign at a RM4M prize pool as
the reference point in that room.

**The case for it.** Saji lives in a kitchen and a cash prize leaves no trace of that. A cheque
handover is one photograph; a kitchen reveal is a series (the before, the build, the reaction, the
first Raya meal cooked in it) and that is content the campaign otherwise has to invent.

**The case against, and what has to be answered first.** One kitchen costs more than several weekly
prizes, so it either converts a block of the 40 or it needs new money. Nobody has a number yet.
Before it goes further someone needs a contractor quote range, a build window that finishes before
the 17 April fulfilment date, and a view on liability if a build goes wrong inside a winner's home.

**Two funding routes if the client bites.** Convert a block of the 40 weekly prizes, holding the
prize pool at RM145,000 and reducing the winner count. Or bring a kitchen or appliance brand in as a
trade sponsor, keeping the winner count and adding a third logo to manage.

**Our recommendation does not change:** 40 weekly winners plus the RM29,000 Golden Ticket.

## 5. QR code versus landing page

Over 90% of Meta and TikTok impressions in Malaysia are mobile in feed. **A QR code in that ad
cannot be scanned by the device showing it.** The viewer has to screenshot it and find a scanner
that reads from the gallery, or find a second device.

What a QR in the ad returns: no click, no pixel, no conversion event, no retargeting pool, no cost
per entry. The platforms never learn who converts, so they keep serving people who never enter. That
is precisely why 2026 bought views rather than entries.

What a landing page returns: the click is recorded, the pixel fires, the conversion event trains the
algorithm, a retargeting pool builds, and cost per entry becomes reportable daily. **That daily
reporting is also the mitigation for the 3.6% risk in section 2**, so it is load bearing rather than
nice to have.

**QR codes keep their place offline**, and the most valuable of those surfaces is the pop-up counter
itself, which catches the shopper with both receipts in hand. It gets its own tracking tag and
should be reported separately. The rest stand: shelf talkers, neck tags, A-boards, block displays,
posters and on pack stickers (2025 printed 10,000 shelf talkers, 7,000 posters and 40,000 neck
tags), boutique counters and packaging inserts, the 150,000 sample packs, the launch at Wisma FGV,
the sampling and roadshow days, and the Sampul Raya. Every code carries its own tag. This is the
AEON model.

## 7. Caveats

1. Are the 40M views and 15M reach KPIs paid only, or campaign wide? The plan assumes campaign wide
   for both. Paid only on views roughly doubles the media requirement.
2. What counts as a view? 2026's 76% view rate implies a 2 second definition, not 30 seconds.
3. Engagement measured on impressions or on reach? The same campaign reads 2.3% or 4.2%.
4. The 2026 spend is unknown, so every CPM in section 2 stays an assumption until the client shares
   it.
5. **3.6% conversion is argued, not measured, and carries no safety margin.** At 3.0% entries land
   at 3,333 against a 4,000 target, with no budget headroom to buy the difference.
6. **Paid reach lands at 13.91M against a briefed 15M.** RM105,000 of working media would clear it.
7. **The pop-up rests on a partner decision we do not control.** Bulan Bintang has to agree to host
   a Saji counter in all 14 boutiques for 10 weeks. If the answer is no, the mechanic reverts to the
   nine step journey and 3.6% is not defensible at all.
8. **No part of the RM145,000 campaign investment covers pop-up cost.** Counter build, stock,
   staffing, the mystery gift and the packaging change sit with on-ground and with the client.
9. **Golden Ticket feasibility**, and the permit scope covering two games of chance. Allow three to
   four weeks from brief to launch on the permit.
10. Chinese New Year on 6 February inflates the auction in contest weeks 1 and 2. A 20 to 40% CPM
    lift in that window is outside our control.
11. Bulan Bintang runs its own QR contest with a RM400,000 cash pool. Confirm whether it overlaps.
12. The client's own slides disagree on the launch date (25 or 28 January) and the winner count (50
    or 53).
13. Website readiness. The page must absorb a weekly announcement spike, so we need to own or
    co-own the hosting and the load test.
14. **Confirm COO and the client are both clear that the prize pool and the campaign investment are
    separate money**, and that total outlay is RM290,000.

## 8b. The access checklist

Deck slide 29. None of it costs money, all of it is permissions and files, and each item carries a
two to three week lead time if it starts late against a page that has to be live on 15 January.

**Platform access**

- Meta Business Manager, partner access to the Saji page and the Instagram account
- Meta ad account admin, plus the Pixel and Conversions API on the contest domain
- TikTok Business Center partner access and TikTok Ads Manager
- TikTok Pixel and Events API, plus Spark Ads codes from every KOL
- Google Ads account linked to our manager account
- YouTube channel manager access, a GA4 property and a Tag Manager container

**Page, domain and data**

- The domain or subdomain the contest page will run on
- DNS and hosting access, SSL, and a load test window before 15 January
- Who owns the entry database, and who is named PDPA data controller
- Who verifies receipts, and the turnaround they commit to
- Who fulfils prizes: the cash, the product and the Golden Ticket

**Brand, partner and legal**

- Brand asset library, including Kak Ji files and product photography
- The qualifying Saji SKU list, and which SKUs carry the Golden Ticket
- The Saji x Bulan Bintang lockup, approved by both sides
- The Bulan Bintang floor space agreement and their 14 store addresses
- Contest permit applicant and filing owner, locked by mid December

**The critical path is two items: pixel access and the domain.** Without them the landing page
cannot report a conversion, and the conversion event is what every measurement argument in the deck
rests on, including the mitigation for the 3.6% risk.

Two lead time notes worth having ready. Google Ads manager linking needs a client side accept. And a
YouTube channel sitting on a personal Google account rather than a brand account is the one that
quietly takes weeks, so ask early which it is.
