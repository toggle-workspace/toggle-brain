# The new silk house, market and positioning research

**Prepared by:** Toggle Solutions for COO
**Date:** 2 September 2026
**Deck:** `new-silk-house-market-and-positioning-research-2026-09-02.pptx`

The client is a new Malaysian silk scarf venture with no brand name yet. It wants to read as high
end, exclusive and locally designed, and its silk is woven in China. COO named Arzu as the reference
the client pointed to. Arzu is treated here as a reference case, not as the client and not as a
target.

Every figure below was read from a public source on 1 or 2 September 2026. Anything we estimated is
labeled as an estimate.

---

## 1. The correction the research makes

The client picked a good reference for the wrong reason. Arzu is worth studying for what it took to
build, not for what it looks like finished.

1. **Arzu's price is carried by time, an address and a founder.** Thirty years of trading, a store
   at the base of the Petronas Towers, and a Turkish-born founder with a story no competitor can
   copy. A brand launching in 2026 can buy none of the three.
2. **Even with all of that, Arzu stops at RM899.** Heritage on its own does not set a price. Arzu
   sells Buy 1 Free 1 at RM69 on the same site.
3. **The blocker for a launch is permission, not sourcing.** Nothing about a brand nobody has heard
   of gives a T20 buyer a reason to spend four figures. Exclusivity is a claim until somebody other
   than the brand confirms it.
4. **Nobody in this market has launched at four figures.** dUCk, Buttonscarves and Naelofar all
   built an audience first and raised the price second. This is the sharpest finding in the research
   and it sits directly against the client's stated ambition.
5. **On origin, say designed in Malaysia and make it true.** Arzu could lean on a line that was
   already accurate. A house launching in 2026 has to earn it with a named designer, a studio
   address and dated original work.

**The specification gap, and why it matters for a launch.** Across Arzu's entire 100% real silk
collection, exactly one product mentions a hand rolled hem (The Good Wrinkle Silk Cotton Long
Scarf, RM249). No product publishes momme weight, dimensions in centimeters, or where the silk was
woven. Sizes are described as "medium" and "large".

Arzu can sell an RM899 square with no specification because it has thirty years standing behind it.
A first edition cannot. Momme, dimensions and hem are the three specifications a buyer researching
silk goes looking for, and a new brand that publishes all three outruns an incumbent that publishes
none, on day one, for the price of a copywriter.

Nothing credible sits between the RM899 Malaysian ceiling and Ferragamo at RM1,002. That gap is the

## 6. Target audience

T20 is an income bracket, not a segment. Three buyer types sit inside it, and for a brand with no
history they do not open in the same order. These come from category evidence, not from client
customer data, which does not exist yet.

| Buyer | Trigger | Position for a launch |
|---|---|---|
| The collector | A drop she was told about before the public, with a number printed on the piece | Hardest to win first. She already collects a brand, and switching costs her the community she is in. |
| The gifter | Raya, a wedding, a farewell for a senior colleague. The gift has to read as expensive on sight. | Winnable cold. She needs the object to look expensive, not the label to be famous. |
| The professional | A wardrobe signal for boardrooms, government events and public appearances | Wins on the cap. She will not wear a print that a hundred other women wore last Raya. |

**Open with the gifter.** Looking expensive on sight is the one job a brand with no reputation can do
in month one. The collector type is directly evidenced by the Duckies and #DuckTower behavior in the
VICE piece, and that loyalty is exactly why she is the hardest to take.

## 7. The origin question

Recommendation: say **designed in Malaysia**, make it true, and never say made in Malaysia.

- Designed in Malaysia is accurate as long as the design work happens here, and it is the same
  construction European houses use when the weaving happens somewhere else.
- The Trade Descriptions Act 2011 makes a false indication of place of manufacture an offence,
  including assisting an act abroad that claims goods were made in Malaysia. A body corporate faces
  up to RM15,000 for each item bearing the false description, rising to RM30,000 for repeat
  offences. ([Act 730 text](https://www.jetro.go.jp/ext_images/world/asia/my/ip/pdf/tradedescription2011_en.pdf),
  [WIPO Lex](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/15309))
- Arzu leans on "Designed In Malaysia Since 1995" because it already was true. A house launching in
  2026 earns that line with a named designer, a studio address and dated original work.

We are not the client's lawyers and should say so. The commercial argument stands on its own: one
screenshot comparing a "made in Malaysia" claim to a Chinese supplier sheet is a brand crisis, and a
new brand has no goodwill to absorb it.

**Open item for COO.** Confirm the design work genuinely happens in Malaysia. If the prints are also
coming off a mill catalog, then "designed in Malaysia" has nothing true underneath it either, and the
first pillar of the proof stack disappears.

## 8. Recommended position

**Launch narrow. Cap the first edition.**

**The first edition.** One silk line in 90x90cm and 110x110cm. Hand rolled hems, momme weight
published, every piece numbered. Priced RM1,200 to RM1,800, into the empty shelf between the RM899
Malaysian ceiling and Ferragamo at RM1,002. Cap the run at 100 to 150 pieces, publish the number,
and never restock it. No promotions, no second line and no restock for twelve months.

**Why the cap carries the price.** At 150 pieces the house does not need a market, it needs 150
buyers, which is one in every ten thousand T20 households. The scarcity is real rather than staged,
and a launch with no warehouse and no wholesale commitments can afford a genuinely small run in a way
an established brand cannot.

**The risk, stated plainly.** No brand in this market has launched at four figures. dUCk took four
years to reach RM800 and five to reach RM1,000, Naelofar was still selling RM73 turbans four years
in, and Buttonscarves started from a home office. Opening at RM1,200 is an exception to the only
pattern the evidence shows. The cap is what makes the exception defensible. If the client wants
volume at this price in year one, this plan is wrong for them and the honest advice is to climb the
way dUCk climbed.

The RM1,200 to RM1,800 band is our judgment call, derived from the gap between the RM899 Malaysian
ceiling and Ferragamo's local entry point. It is not a published figure.

### What to publish at launch

1. **Momme weight.** Twelve momme and above is the number a buyer who has researched silk goes
   looking for, and its absence reads as an answer.
2. **Dimensions.** State 90x90cm and 110x110cm on every page. The category sells "medium" and
   "large", which tells a serious buyer nothing.
3. **The hem.** Hand rolled and hand stitched, photographed close. The most visible difference
   between a RM150 scarf and a RM1,500 one.
4. **The edition.** Print a number on the piece and the box. "Piece 24 of 150" turns a purchase into
   something worth keeping and photographing.

Cost: copywriting and photography, not manufacturing. It is free here, because there is no legacy
catalog to go back and fix.

### The proof stack

Exclusivity is confirmed by other people. These four assets make the confirmation possible without
waiting thirty years for it, and none of them needs a loom in Malaysia.

| Asset | Starts working | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|
| An original print archive | Immediately | Prints the house draws and owns. The only defense against a mill selling the same square to somebody else, and it compounds every season. |
| A published edition cap | At launch | A number on the piece and the box, honored in public. Worth nothing if it is quietly extended, worth a lot the first time it sells out. |
| A named designer | In months | A face, a studio and a reason she draws what she draws. Buyers cannot check momme weight from a photograph. They can check a person. |
| One physical room | Within a year | Appointment viewing, a counter or a short residency. Arzu bought this signal with a KLCC address. A residency rents it by the month. |

The print archive is the priority. Hangzhou mills quote custom squares with no minimum order, which
means the design file is the only thing a competitor cannot copy legally. A house that sells a mill
catalog print at RM1,400 has bought a screenshot and a lawsuit instead of a brand.

## 9. Online sales method

The mechanics are the same ones dUCk proved. The order is what changes for a launch, because dUCk had
FashionValet's customer list before it ever ran a limited edition.

| Step | Mechanism | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Build the list | Six to nine months of the designer's face, the prints and the studio. Paid media does the finding, because a new name has no organic reach. Collect phone numbers, not followers. | dUCk launched under FashionValet, which already held the list ([FashionValet](https://www.fashionvalet.com/duck/about-us.html)) |
| 2. Then drop | Announce the date, cap the run, publish the count, never restock. A real cap makes the scarcity true rather than theatrical. | dUCk proved the mechanic on this audience at this price ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/luxury-hijab-malaysia-wealth-status-malaysia/)) |
| 3. Sell on WhatsApp | Named advisor, early previews, order updates by hand, Chat to Buy in place of Add to Cart. | WhatsApp reaches 98% of Malaysian social users ([Marketing Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/whatsapp-social-commerce-analysis)); luxury clienteling patterns ([Endear](https://endearhq.com/blog/clienteling-in-luxury-retail), [Merx](https://www.hellomerx.com/blog/use-cases-whatsapp-clienteling-software-for-luxury-retail)); Chat to Buy for high ticket in Malaysia ([Blaster Pro](https://www.blaster-pro.com/post/how-to-build-a-whatsapp-first-brand-lessons-from-malaysia-s-top-5-viral-startups)) |

**On paid media.** Buy the audience, then stop. Advertising a capped edition after it opens tells
buyers it did not sell.

**On TikTok.** Malaysia grew TikTok Shop GMV 150%, the fastest in the region, and live sessions
convert at 5% to 20% ([TNGlobal](https://technode.global/2026/02/11/tiktoks-southeast-asia-doubles-gmv-year-on-year-to-45-6b-in-2025/),
[ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/tiktok-shop-strategy-southeast-asia/)). It is the right
channel for a RM200 to RM500 tier and the wrong one for a capped first edition. Recommend it in year
two if the house adds a lower line, and keep the flagship off live selling either way.

## 10. Talking points for the call

**"Our silk comes from China."** So does most of the silk in this category. Buyers are asking who
drew the print and how many exist, not where the loom sits. Publish the design story and the edition
count, and say designed in Malaysia, which stays true as long as the design happens here.

**"We want to open at RM1,500."** You can, if the first edition is 150 pieces and not 1,500. At that
size the brand needs 150 buyers, not a market. Ask for volume at that price in year one and the plan
breaks. Deliver this one carefully. It decides whether the engagement is worth taking, and saying so
now is cheaper than saying it in month six.

**"Arzu does it, so we can too."** Arzu has thirty years, a KLCC address and a founder story, and its
price still stops at RM899. Copying the angle without the proof leaves a RM1,400 scarf with nothing
holding it up.

**"Can we say made in Malaysia?"** No. The Trade Descriptions Act 2011 puts up to RM15,000 an item
behind that sentence. Designed in Malaysia does the same work and survives a screenshot.

**Close on this.** The mills will sell anybody the same silk. They cannot sell them the person who
drew the print.

## 11. Risks and verification gaps

**Gaps.** Buttonscarves silk square pricing in ringgit is unconfirmed, their collection pages
returned errors on every attempt, and visible new arrivals sat near RM115. Beyond that, the brand
does not exist yet: no name, no founder story, no confirmed design capability in Malaysia, no unit
cost and no launch date. Each of those moves the plan.

**Risks.** A published edition that fails to sell out is worse than never publishing one, so the
first run should be sized against the list built before launch rather than against the ambition. The
Hangzhou mills sell to anyone with a design file, so an archive of original prints is the only
durable defense against dupes.

Pricing advice here assumes healthy silk margins at the RM1,200 to RM1,800 band. Given a landed cost
near the USD 28 to 52 direct band, that assumption is safe, but it should be confirmed against the
client's actual quote.
