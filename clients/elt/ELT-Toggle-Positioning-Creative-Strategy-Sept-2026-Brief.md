# Build brief

## Decision this deck exists to win

Yuli named creative strategy as the bottleneck, not budget and not channel setup. The deck has to
agree with that diagnosis, prove it with her own channel data, then sell the positioning work that
fixes it. It opens as a consulting engagement, scaling into management later.

## Positioning territory chosen

**"Built for the weather, not the podium."** Selected over two alternatives (chef-made taste as the
lead, and the recreational-player audience as the lead) because it has the highest ceiling, supports
the daily habit the 65% subscription target depends on, and cannot be followed by any competitor
without them abandoning their athlete rosters.

Chef-made taste demotes to the first reason to believe. The recreational player and the sideline
parent become the audience definition rather than the position.

## Deliberate omission

**The 4.9 star rating does not appear anywhere in the deck.** Yuli already knows the product is
good, and a rating does not sell a position. The 30% organic reorder rate replaces it as the proof
point throughout, because it is behavioral rather than declarative.

## The creative angle section, rebuilt

Yi Yang flagged that the original slides 5 to 8 asserted the angles without showing the mechanism
connecting position to ad, and without data justifying why any angle should work. Rebuilt as five
slides:

**Slide 6 is new and carries the section.** A Thailand study of 166 recreational runners at 29.6°C
and 70% humidity, conditions almost identical to Kuala Lumpur, measured sodium loss at 54 mmol an
hour for men, which is 1,240mg. ELT returns 600mg a sachet. 100Plus Zero returns 80mg a can. So
replacing one hour of sweat takes two sachets or fifteen and a half cans. That single comparison
makes the position physiological rather than rhetorical, and no competitor is teaching it.

**Slide 8 is the flow Yi Yang asked for.** Three lanes across a cold, warm and repeat axis, showing
that the sodium angle owns cold, the heat angle spans cold and warm, and the taste angle spans warm
and repeat. Each stage carries its own metric. The angles relay rather than compete.

**Slide 9 makes the angles concrete**, with the hook we would run, the evidence under it, and the
number it answers to.

### Claims risk, and the rewrite that resolves it

The evidence linking muscle cramps to electrolyte loss is contested. Cramps occur without
electrolyte depletion, and the current lean is toward neuromuscular fatigue (Sports Medicine, 2019).
Yuli's best-performing angle rests on that contested claim.

The recommendation is not to drop the angle but to move the claim: assert the sweat sodium loss,
which is measured, rather than the cramp cure, which is not. The emotional payoff is identical
(the heat took something from you, you are not unfit) and the rewritten claim survives a Meta
health-claim review and Malaysian advertising standards. This sits on slide 9 as the verdict.

### The second wave, slide 10

Yi Yang asked for a second angle set on the slide 8 lane pattern. Wave one sequences the three
angles Yuli already runs. Wave two is the set she does not, and it is what justifies the asset
counts on slide 11 and opens the parent lane on slide 12.

| Stage | Hook | Why it is new | Data anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold and warm | "You packed the water. You forgot the salt." | A new audience, the sideline parent, who buys for two and buys on a schedule | 143,000 pickleball players and no competitor addressing the parent |
| Warm | "RM11 a sachet, or RM5.40." | A new intent pool, the LMNT switcher already searching, with halal as the closer | 81% of Malaysians say halal certification drives their purchase, and 63% of the country is Muslim |
| Repeat | "Never open the last sachet." | A new revenue model, converting reorders into subscription | 30% already reorder unprompted against 10 subscribers today |

The lane shape deliberately differs from slide 8. Wave one is an ascending staircase (cold, cold
and warm, warm and repeat). Wave two descends (cold and warm, warm, repeat), so the two slides read
as different systems rather than a repeated template.

**Flag on the halal angle:** drinkelt.com says "halal-certified ingredients", which is not the same
as JAKIM product certification. Confirm which one ELT holds before putting spend behind the switch
angle. This sits on slide 10 as the caveat.

### Format evidence

Creator-led, face-to-camera beats product-only by 2.3x on hold rate. UGC hook rate runs 34% against
26% for studio, and UGC costs roughly a fifth as much per asset. Polished studio only wins on brand
recall and on items above USD 200, so at a RM120 cart it does not pay for itself. This drives the
format column on slide 8.

## The finding that carries the deck

ROAS equals average cart divided by cost per purchase, so Yuli's two stated targets are
arithmetically incompatible. At the RM120 cart she quoted, a sub-RM50 cost per purchase produces
2.4x, not the 4x to 5x she wants. Reaching 4x at RM120 requires a RM30 cost per purchase. Holding
RM50 and reaching 4x requires a RM200 cart.

Recommendation on slide 11: move both levers. Cost per purchase to RM35 to RM40, cart to RM150 to
RM170, landing ROAS between 3.8x and 4.9x.

## House style compliance

10 x 5.625in, 16:9. Arial only. Title 26pt bold at (0.55, 0.34), subtitle 13pt grey at 0.87,
hairline #DDE5E3 at 1.26, content band 1.5 to 4.9, footer 9pt at 5.21 with page number right.
Five colours with fixed jobs: #14201F the claim, #5B6B6A the caveat, #A9B5B4 de-emphasis,
#0295AD Toggle and the conservative case, #C07016 the alternate and aggressive case.
Zero bullet characters. Zero speaker notes. Every number carries its source. Ranges, not point
estimates. Ends on an ask.

Prose runs 85 words a slide against the 59 benchmark measured on the Ikonik forecast deck.
Positioning statements and competitor evidence carry more text than a chart caption does. Table
cells add a further 25 a slide and are data rather than prose.
