Targeting and placements

Audiences built for parents and applicants, with drift checked every week

Sedifly's note on the previous campaign is the brief for this page: its targeting leaned toward B2B and C-suite audiences, and the team had little view of lead quality or conversions. Four controls answer the first half of that, and the weekly report answers the second.

1 · Age and location are hard limits

Every ad set runs in Singapore only, with fixed age bands: 38 to 58 for parents and 18 to 24 for applicants. We switch Advantage+ audience off so Meta cannot widen the bands on its own. Meta does not allow ads to target anyone under 18, so we reach students in Sec 4 to JC2 through their parents.

2 · We leave out job title, industry and income targeting

Professional and income signals are the most likely source of the B2B and C-suite drift. Inside the age bands we keep detailed targeting open and let two things do the sorting: creative that names the reader's situation, and form questions that ask who is filling the form in.

3 · Placements match the assets

We run Instagram and Facebook Feeds, Stories and Reels only. Audience Network, Messenger and the right column stay off, because those placements produce the cheapest and weakest form fills. The 9:16 videos and statics serve Stories and Reels; the 4:5 statics and the carousel serve Feeds.

4 · Exclusions keep cold budget on new families

Current clients, past enquirers and anyone already booked in HubSpot are excluded from both cold campaigns. Case study leads move out of Campaign 2 the day they convert and into the retargeting pool, so Sedifly does not pay twice to reach the same family.

How Sedifly will see lead quality

Every Monday report shows each ad set's leads by age, by the first form answer (parent, student, or student with a parent involved) and by HubSpot score. If more than 30% of an ad set's leads in a week fall outside the parent or applicant profile, we tighten that ad set the same week and record the change in the report.

Creative review 1 of 2 · The adapted organic Reel

The Ke Yue Reel has a strong hook, but the offer arrives at 40 seconds

44.7 seconds · 9:16 · captions burned in · CTA: Readiness Check
What works
  • The opening line, "What do you do when your dream school rejects you?", lands in the first two seconds and names the viewer's situation.
  • Ke Yue tells her own story: no top international school, a gap year, and advice that she was aiming too high. The UChicago letter at 37 seconds is proof a competitor cannot copy.
  • Burned-in captions carry the story for viewers who watch with the sound off.
Where it runs

The Reel speaks to a student who has been rejected or is on a gap year, so it leads in the applicant ad set. In the parent ad set it runs with parent-facing primary text, for example "If your child's first round did not go to plan." Its strongest window opens in mid-December, when US Early Decision results arrive.

Recommended editWhy
1. Show "Free Admissions Readiness Check" on screen from about 10 seconds, and again when the UChicago letter appears.The call to action first appears at 40 seconds of 45. Most Reels viewers leave well before the end, so most will not see the ask in the current cut. Please also confirm the Readiness Check is free, since the site does not say so.
2. Cut to about 30 seconds by removing the white text cards at 24 to 27 seconds and trimming the "12 months of watching" section. Export a 15-second version as well: hook, gap year line, letter, CTA.A shorter cut keeps more viewers until the proof. The 15-second version serves Stories and retargeting.
3. Label the letter shot with "Offer: University of Chicago".The letter is on screen for about a second, and the seal alone is easy to miss. The outcome is the point of the Reel.
4. Move the "AN UNDERDOG STORY" label from the bottom fifth of the frame into the top third.On Reels, the caption, account name and button cover the bottom of the screen.
5. Open the end card with the Readiness Check line already on it.The final card shows for half a second before the call to action appears.
Test budget: about S$250 over the first 14 days, inside the Readiness Check campaign across both ad sets. At day 14 we keep and scale the Reel if its hook rate (3-second views over impressions) is 25% or higher and its cost per enquiry is at or under S$55. Day 30 confirms its cost per qualified enquiry. If Sedifly adds budget after that, a winning Reel takes it first.

Creative review 2 of 2 · The Joash interview ad

The Joash interview carries founder authority, and it needs a result and the right CTA

29.4 seconds · 9:16 · captions burned in · CTA: free consultation
What works
  • Joash speaks to camera, with a name and title lower third from the fourth second. A cold parent gets a face and a role to trust.
  • The message, that admissions officers read the applicant's story and contribution as well as grades, sets up the job the Readiness Check does.
Where it runs

It runs in the parent ad set of the Readiness Check campaign and in retargeting. Founder authority works hardest on people who have already seen Sedifly once, so retargeting is where we expect it to earn most.

Recommended editWhy
1. Open on Joash speaking, with "250+ offers from top US and UK universities" as text over the shot.The first 2.5 seconds are a still green card. Those seconds decide whether a viewer stops scrolling, and a face holds attention better than a number on a plain background.
2. Confirm the 250+ figure before launch.sedifly.com and the ad copy approved in October both say 150+. The ads and the site need to state the same number, and Meta can ask for proof of a results claim.
3. Add one named result before the end card, for example Ke Yue going from an unsuccessful first cycle to UChicago.The ad is briefed as results-led, yet the script explains how admissions work and shows no student outcome.
4. Change the end card from "Book a free consultation" to "Take the free Admissions Readiness Check".The form, the HubSpot stage and the other ads all use the Readiness Check name. One name for one offer keeps the promise and the reporting consistent.
5. Fix the caption overlap at about 17 seconds, where two lines blend into "do wiing into college".Viewers watching with the sound off read every caption.
6. Confirm the interview's producer allows paid use, and how to credit it.The Acumen and Davos Interviews backdrop is on screen throughout. Once cleared, a short "As featured on" credit at the start adds credibility.

Ke Yue case study campaign · New

The Ke Yue case study is a free download that feeds the Readiness Check

A case study lead sits earlier in the decision than a Readiness Check enquiry. We judge this campaign on how many of its leads score A or B and go on to book, and its cost per download comes second.

Creative read

We expect "86.65 RP. 1490 SAT. Then she reapplied." and "Her profile wasn't weak." to lead, because both give a Singapore parent specifics to compare with their own child. The carousel suits Feeds, where its order (starting profile, diagnosis, changes, offers) rewards the swipe. The Hero static has no button and no number, so it runs as the control.

Fix before launch

  • SAT score: the ads say 1490, and the case study page shown in the eBook mockup says 1390. One of them needs correcting.
  • Typo: the last carousel card reads "Once Cycle Later" in both the 4:5 and 1:1 sets. It should read "One Cycle Later".
  • 9:16 safe zones: on three of the four 9:16 statics the logo sits in the top tenth of the frame, and the profile reveal button sits in the bottom tenth with its arrow wrapped to a second line. Instagram's Stories and Reels interface covers both areas.
  • The PDF: we need the 4-page case study itself, hosted at a link the form and the HubSpot email can open.
Month-one targets: S$15 to S$25 per case study lead (24 to 40 leads), 40% to 55% of leads scored A or B, and 10% to 15% of leads submitting a Readiness Check within 30 days.

Assumptions

Tracking, reporting and optimization

Every lead is tracked from the form to the consultation

1Form

Each campaign has its own Instant Form, so every HubSpot contact shows which campaign it came from.

2HubSpot

The lead arrives in real time through HubSpot's Meta Lead Ads sync. Toggle's lead sheet also records its campaign, ad set and ad.

3Score

The three form answers score the lead A to D. A and B count as qualified (HubSpot stage MQL).

4Booked and attended

Sedifly marks the Readiness Check booked (SQL) and attended (Opportunity) in HubSpot within 24 hours.

5Back to Meta

HubSpot's Conversions API sync sends qualified, booked and attended events to Meta, which shows cost per qualified enquiry in Ads Manager.
ReportingWhat Sedifly gets
Weekly, every Monday from 16 NovemberOne page: spend, enquiries, qualified, booked and attended by campaign; cost per qualified enquiry; ads kept and cut; the audience quality breakdown; and the one decision we took that week, with the reason.
Day 14, Tuesday 24 NovemberThe creative call: the Reel against the Joash interview, and the leading Ke Yue statics.
Day 30, Thursday 10 DecemberA KPI review against this forecast, with the initial KPIs reset on actual results.

Optimization criteria

  1. Days 1 to 7: no budget or targeting changes unless something breaks (tracking, a disapproval, or zero delivery).
  2. Ads: we retire a video with a hook rate under 20% after 2,000 impressions, and a static with a link click-through rate under 1.0% after 1,500 impressions.
  3. Ad sets: above S$55 per enquiry cold, S$38 in retargeting or S$25 per case study lead for five straight days, we rotate creative first and then cut budget.
  4. Quality over volume: an ad or ad set with fewer than 35% of its leads qualified after 10 leads loses budget, even when its cost per enquiry looks cheap.
  5. Reallocation: from week three, a campaign holding cost per qualified enquiry 20% under the S$143 ceiling for seven days can take up to S$200 a month from the weakest one. Every move appears in that week's report.
  6. Optimizing for quality: once the account has about 100 qualified events, we test Meta's Conversion Leads goal so Meta bids for qualified leads instead of form fills.
