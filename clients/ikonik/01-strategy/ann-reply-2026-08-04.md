### IKONIK WhatsApp review

Hi Ann,

Per your ask, we went through the WhatsApp side ourselves. We went in as a patient rather than as an agency, so what follows is what an actual enquiry runs into.

One thing worth saying up front: your team is not the problem. When a human picked up, the reply came in about three minutes, which is genuinely good for a clinic. Everything below is about the automated layer sitting in front of them.

**The short version: about 7 minutes, about 4 taps, and no price.**

#### What we observed

**The reply came back in the wrong language, twice.** We opened in English. The flow replied in Malay. We then asked, in plain English, whether it could reply in English, and it sent the identical Malay message again, word for word.

**The flow is built entirely on button menus,** so anything a patient types in free text goes unanswered. That includes the three questions that most often decide a booking: how much is LASIK for both eyes, am I eligible at my prescription, and do you treat children.

**The RM2,500 per eye promotion is running on TikTok and in your website popup, but the WhatsApp flow that receives those clicks is not wired to that price,** so it cannot confirm it when a patient asks. Worth confirming that is still your live offer, since we are reading it off the site.

**The chat stops working on desktop.** Partway through, a step fails to render in the browser, so anyone enquiring from a laptop reaches a dead end at the exact moment they were ready to book.

**In the conversation we traced, nothing was captured** before a human had to take over manually. No name, no age, no prescription, no preferred date. The human then started the conversation over from the beginning.

**A booking cannot currently be traced back to the ad that produced it.** Your website form asks patients how they heard about you. The WhatsApp flow has no equivalent field, so spend cannot be tuned toward whatever is actually working.

#### Two things we want to check next

We would rather tell you what we have not tested than imply we tested everything.

**Out-of-hours handling.** The closing message tells the patient a live agent will contact them shortly. We have not yet tested what happens when that message goes out at 9pm on a weekday or on a Sunday, when the clinic is closed. If there is no one staffed behind it at those hours, that matters, because it is a promise going out automatically.

**Urgent symptoms.** We want to check whether a patient describing a red, painful eye gets any different handling from someone asking about a promotion. On the current menu structure we could not see a path that would treat them differently, but we have not confirmed it, and it is the first thing we would want to fix if true.

#### How the replacement would work

We build this on respond.io, using an AI agent rather than a button menu. Concretely:

**It reads sentences instead of taps.** Malay, English and Chinese, detected from the patient's first message and held for the rest of the conversation. If someone switches language mid-chat, it follows.

**It answers the money questions on the spot, from a fixed list you approve.** RM2,500 per eye, RM5,000 for both, final price confirmed after assessment. It cannot invent a number, offer a discount, or quote a price you have not approved, including under pressure.

**It captures a real record.** Name, age band, service, number and preferred day are written to a contact record before a human opens the chat, so your team starts from a qualified conversation instead of from zero.

**It works at 10pm.** It collects everything and tells the patient honestly when the team will call, rather than promising someone immediately.

**Urgent symptoms are pulled out of the sales flow first.** A defined list of red flags routes straight to a human and stops the booking questions, rather than queuing behind a menu.

**It routes by age.** Children go to Ortho-K, adults to laser, and the over-50s to cataract or lens options, which is the same structure your own website already uses.

**Attribution comes from the ad, not from the patient.** Once the click-to-WhatsApp ads are set up to pass the click reference through, the ad is recorded automatically against the contact, the same way your website form records "how did you hear about us."

We have already written this agent, including the Malay, English and Chinese handling and the clinical triage rules. It is not deployed, and there are a handful of things only you can confirm before it could be, including whether the RM2,500 promo is current and who would staff the urgent queue.

#### Next step

Would a short call make sense to walk through this? We would rather show you the replacement agent working than describe it.

Thanks again for the opening to look at this.

Best,
Zaid

## Before you send this

**Run the after-hours test first.** It costs two WhatsApp messages and about a minute. Send "Hi, I want to book a LASIK consultation" to their line at around 9:30pm tonight, and again on Sunday. If nobody replies until the next working morning, the out-of-hours point moves from "something we want to check" into a hard finding with a timestamp on it, and it becomes the strongest item in the whole document. Right now I have written it honestly as an open question, because we have not run it.

**The same applies to the urgent-symptom test.** Sending "Mata saya merah dan sakit sejak semalam, patut saya datang sekarang?" would settle it. If a human picks it up, tell them it was a test so you are not burning clinical attention.

**Three things would make this land much harder, and I could not write them for you:**

1. **Two screenshots**, the Malay-repeated-after-asking-for-English exchange and the desktop error. You already have both. A screenshot removes any need for adjectives.
2. **A cost figure using their real numbers.** If you know roughly what they spend on TikTok and how many click-to-WhatsApp clicks that buys, one honest sentence turns this from a user-experience critique into a business case. I did not invent one.
3. **Process-based social proof, if we have it.** Something like "we run this same triage-and-capture setup for another healthcare client, live since X". Do not use a results number with this client, since an agency's traffic promise is exactly what burned them before.

**What I deliberately left out.** I cut a line claiming their out-of-hours gap overlaps with TikTok LIVE's best performing window. It is a reasonable hypothesis and it is in our internal notes, but we have no data for it, and Ann may know it to be wrong. Claims like that are cheap to make and expensive to be corrected on.

**Tone note.** You asked for language that shows the current setup is bad. I kept it diplomatic on purpose, and I would push back once on going harder. This client was burned by an agency that oversold with confident, aggressive language. Blunt criticism from the next agency pattern-matches to that same voice, even when it is accurate. The evidence here is damning enough without adjectives: about 7 minutes, about 4 taps, no price, and the same message sent twice to someone who asked for English. Let the numbers carry it.
