Patient chat review

How an enquiry actually gets handled: IKONIK and OasisEye, side by side

You asked to see how your chat compares to OasisEye's. We ran both ourselves, as a patient rather than as an agency, and asked each of them the same ordinary questions.

Prepared by Toggle Solutions for IKONIK Eye Specialist Centre · 4 August 2026

The first thing we noticed

OasisEye has a chat on their website. IKONIK has none.

A visitor on ikonik-eyecentre.com has three ways to make contact: a phone number, an email address, and a form. There is no chat widget and no WhatsApp link anywhere on the site. Someone reading about LASIK at 10pm has nothing to tap.

So the first gap is not about quality. It is about presence. They are in the conversation and you are not.

In the two sessions we ran, neither clinic gave a price. Yours is promoting RM2,500 per eye on TikTok and in the website popup, so the answer exists inside the business. It is just not reachable from the channel the ads point at.

Both chats also sent the same canned message a second time rather than responding to what the patient had just written.

What OasisEye gets right

The format is the right one

Their replies are written in full sentences and read like a person. There are no button menus and no "choose option 1 or 2." Next to a rigid template, it feels like talking to a clinic, and patients respond to that.

If that is what you like about their chat, we agree with you.

It did not hold up for six minutes

In the session we captured, three patient messages went unanswered for about four minutes, and the same automated greeting fired a second time. The price question was asked twice and not answered.

We cannot tell from the outside which parts of their chat are automated and which are a person typing. What we can see is what the patient experienced.

The opportunity is to take the format they got right and make it hold.

A patient should get that same natural conversation, and get it at 2am, in the language they wrote in, with prices you have approved. That is the gap between the two columns, and neither clinic has closed it yet.

What we would do first

01

Put a chat on the website

There is none today, which is the clearest gap against OasisEye. The same agent answers on WhatsApp, so both channels behave identically.

02

Load the answers you approve

Prices, procedures, hours, and what each technology covers. The agent cannot invent a figure or offer a discount, including under pressure from a patient.

03

Set the urgent-symptom rules

A defined list of red flags leaves the booking flow and goes straight to a person. This one needs a decision from you about who receives them and how quickly, because routing urgent cases into an unwatched inbox would be worse than not routing them at all.

04

Connect the ads to the record

Once click-to-WhatsApp is wired up, every enquiry carries the ad that produced it, so spend can be pointed at whatever is actually booking patients.

We have already written the agent, in Malay, English and Chinese, including the clinical triage rules. We would rather show it to you working than describe it.
