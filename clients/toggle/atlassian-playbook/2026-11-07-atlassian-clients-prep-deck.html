The basics

An Atlassian app makes money when a Jira team tries it, pays for it and keeps paying

Steps 1 to 3 bring evaluations, and steps 4 and 5 bring revenue. Use these steps when you talk about either goal on the call.

1
A Jira team searches

They search on Google, inside the Marketplace or in AI tools, or they see our ad.

→
2
They land on the listing

The vendor's website cannot sell a cloud app, so every path ends on the Marketplace listing.

→
3
They start an evaluation

A Jira admin clicks Try. Anyone else clicks Request, which asks their admin to install it.

→
4
The trial ends and they pay

The price follows the total number of users on the Jira site, including people who never open the app.

→
5
They renew and add apps

Renewals, bigger teams and a second app from the same vendor all add revenue.

Goal 1 · More evaluationsOur ads, pages and listing work move steps 1 to 3.
Goal 2 · More revenueSite size, upgrades and cross-sells drive steps 4 and 5, and the vendor controls most of them.
Bigger Jira sites pay far more

Deep Clone costs $665 a year for 50 users and $6,910 for 1,000 users. One large customer is worth ten small ones, so targeting larger companies is a revenue lever.

Ad platforms count clicks on Try

Google Ads and GA4 count clicks on Try, Request and Install. The vendor's Marketplace report shows which of those clicks became real evaluations.

A cookie banner hides most ad visitors

Since 12 June 2026, Atlassian's banner hides visitors in regulated regions such as Europe who decline cookies. In October, GA4 saw 24 of the 195 people who clicked ij-solutions' ads.
