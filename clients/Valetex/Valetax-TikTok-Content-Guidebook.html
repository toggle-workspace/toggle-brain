Section 01 · The stakes

01  Why this category is different

Most brands can post first and refine later — Valetax cannot. Leveraged forex/CFDs sit in the single most-restricted bucket of paid social, and the advertiser account already carries history. A single careless video does not merely underperform: it adds a violation, degrades account health, and can trigger a suspension that takes an entire market offline.

3+1
priority markets (ID, MY, TH) can't currently run ads — plus VN, treated as prohibited (see §7)
2
TikTok rulebooks apply to a broker: the paid Ads policy and the Branded-Content ban
0
tolerance for guarantees — "no risk mentioned" is a documented violation on its own

Three factors working against us

  • The product is restricted by default. TikTok treats CFDs / leveraged trading as "complex speculative products" — gated on local licensing, prohibited outright in several markets.
  • The account has a record. Valetax is on Malaysia's SC Investor Alert List, and the advertiser account already carries rejections. Reviewers apply a higher standard, not a lower one.
  • Reviewers see everything. The video, the caption, and the landing page are all scanned. A clean video pointing at a non-compliant page still fails.

What actually moves us forward

  • Licensing first. The durable fix in every market is a licensed entity + business verification — content discipline buys time and protects the brand, it doesn't replace the licence.
  • Durable compliance beats short-lived cleverness. A feature demonstration that runs for months outperforms a viral hook that is pulled within a day.
  • One reviewable system. Same disclaimer, same checklist, same vetting on every asset — so approvals become predictable rather than arbitrary.
Section 02 · The principle

02  The core shift, and five rules under it

Every problem in the audit traces back to one habit: selling the outcome of trading. The fix is a single change of subject — stop selling returns, start selling the platform and the experience of using it. Hold that line and the remainder of this guidebook is simply detail.

The one move
"10% Profit · 54% Growth"→"Trade the markets on a platform built for it."

Sell the product (tools, speed, access, education), never the payoff (profit, growth, winnings). The payoff is exactly what regulators and TikTok's AI are trained to catch.

The five rules that follow from it. These are the common ground across the UK FCA, EU/ESMA, Singapore MAS and Australia's ASIC — i.e. the standard every serious reviewer applies. Treat them as non-negotiable on every asset.

1 · Fair, clear, not misleading

Judged on the first-impression of the whole video — not the fine print. If a casual viewer could walk away thinking "easy money," it fails, regardless of disclaimers.

2 · Risk gets equal prominence

Any benefit/feature claim must be matched by a visible, legible risk warning at the same level — not a caption, not an end-card, not "see more…". On short video, keep it on-screen.

3 · No guarantees, ever

No promised, implied, "risk-free," "fixed," or "fast" returns. No profit figures as outcomes. No "turn $X into $Y." This is the most heavily penalised claim type in finance advertising.

4 · No inducements

Deposit bonuses, welcome credits, rebates, refer-a-friend cash, and trading contests / prize mechanics are banned or heavily restricted for CFDs across regulated markets. The same applies to "win" promotions.

5 · Own your creators

Any paid post must be disclosed plainly — and Valetax stays liable for what they say. An unvetted creator's claim becomes Valetax's violation.

Section 03 · Green-light

03  What to produce

There is a genuine, brand-building lane here; it is simply not the returns lane. Make content about the platform, the experience, and the education, always carrying the risk disclaimer. These formats are the safest path to ads that approve and stay live.

Platform & feature demos

Show the app doing its job: fast order execution, clean charts, the deposit and withdrawal flow, tight spreads, multi-device access. Screen-record the real product. Describe features factually — "place a trade in three taps," never "profit in three taps."

"Trading-on-the-go" lifestyle

The accessibility angle from the audit: trading from your phone, between meetings, on the move. Keep the lifestyle about convenience and control, never about the wealth it purportedly buys (no cars, cash stacks, or luxury).

Education & literacy

Explain what a spread is, how leverage amplifies losses as well as gains, how to read a candlestick, risk-management basics. Educational, balanced content is the lowest-risk format and builds genuine authority.

Brand & trust signals

Who Valetax is, the support team, security of funds, the breadth of markets, years operating. Brand-led content that makes no performance claim is the easiest to clear and the most durable.

Product news, factually

New instruments, platform updates, supported languages and regions, app improvements. State what's new and what it does — leave out any implication of what users will earn.

Welcome offers — proceed with caution

The audit's "Up to 300 in rewards" idea is an inducement, which regulated markets restrict for CFDs. If used at all: only where lawful, never tied to trading volume, framed as account onboarding (not a payoff), with full T&Cs on the landing page — and expect heightened scrutiny. The default is to omit it. The dividing line from the banned reward-for-trading in §4 is simple: an onboarding gift not tied to depositing or trading sits here in amber; the moment it's linked to volume or deposits, it's red.

The shape of a safe video

Hook on the brand

Lead with the product or a genuine question ("What's a spread?"), not a promise of money. Favour substance over hook-bait.

Show the product

Real app footage or a clear explainer. Features and access: the capabilities customers actually use.

Disclaimer throughout

Risk disclaimer on-screen and legible for the duration (see Section 5), plus full T&Cs on the landing page.

Section 04 · Red-light

04  What never to include

These are the things that get videos rejected, ad groups marked "financial misrepresentation," and accounts suspended. Each one below is drawn from the audit and confirmed against TikTok and financial-regulator policy. Treat the list as absolute: if an asset contains any of these, it does not ship.

From your own audit — the five issues that prompted this guidance Performance figures ("10% Profit / 54% Growth"), gambling-style assets (the "JACKLOT" contest), reward-for-trading mechanics, missing risk disclosure, and ad/landing-page mismatch. Every one maps to a hard rule below.
×
Profit, growth, or return figures as outcomes. "10% Profit," "54% Growth," "+$348 today," win-rate percentages, profit screenshots. These read as a guaranteed or exaggerated financial claim, the most heavily penalised claim type in finance advertising, on TikTok and with every regulator.
×
Any guarantee or "risk-free / easy / fast money" framing. "Earn while you sleep," "double your money," "turn $100 into $1,000," countdowns and FOMO urgency. Implied certainty is treated the same as a stated guarantee.
×
Gambling-style or contest assets. The "JACKLOT Global Trading Contest" lottery/jackpot aesthetic, "win," prize-draw, leaderboard, or any framing that presents trading as a game of chance. This is both a gambling-content problem and a banned inducement.
×
Reward-for-trading inducements. Gifts, cash, Xu/credits, or rebates tied to opening, depositing, or trading volume. Deposit-match bonuses and refer-a-friend cash are restricted/banned for CFDs across the FCA, ESMA, and ASIC regimes.
×
Lifestyle-flex bait. Luxury cars, cash fans, watches, mansions, "quit your 9-to-5." Showing or "flaunting money" is explicitly disallowed and signals get-rich-quick.
×
Benefits with no balancing risk. Any upside claim that isn't matched by a visible, legible risk warning. "Misleading because it does not mention risk" is a documented violation by itself.
×
Past performance without context + warning. Historical returns, "last month's trades," equity curves — only ever with a "past performance is not a reliable indicator of future performance" line, on the same screen, in the regulator-mandated format for that market [VERIFY]. Better still, avoid past-performance content on paid social entirely.
×
Copy-trading / "follow top-performing traders" framing (the CopyTrade reel). Presenting other people's results as the outcome you can expect is past-performance-as-promise — restricted the same way as a profit claim. Describe the copy feature factually ("mirror a strategy you choose") without implying their gains are yours, and never pair it with returns.
×
"Regulated / safe / licensed" claims based on the offshore licence. An offshore registration (e.g. Valetax's offshore licence [VERIFY jurisdiction]) confers no legality in ID/MY/TH/VN, and asserting "regulated/safe" there is itself misleading. Never imply endorsement by SC, MAS, OJK, BAPPEBTI, SEC, or BOT, and never use their names/logos.
×
Paid-creator "Branded Content" for trading. TikTok bans the Branded-Content / paid-partnership toggle for forex, CFDs, and crypto globally, even when licensed. Promote only through verified, whitelisted paid ads — never the creator branded-content path.
×
Ad / landing-page mismatch — or swapping the page after approval. The destination must carry the same offer, full T&Cs, and risk disclosure as the ad. Changing the page post-approval is treated as deceptive and gets accounts suspended.
×
New ad accounts to dodge a rejection or appeal. Spinning up fresh accounts while flagged or appealing worsens the case and risks broader bans. Fix the content and work the existing account.
Section 05 · The disclaimer

05  The mandatory risk disclaimer

Every Valetax ad needs a risk disclaimer that is on-screen, legible, and present for long enough to read — and the same disclosures must live on the landing page. A disclaimer cannot rescue a misleading video, but its absence is a violation in itself. Below is a working base; each region needs its own counsel-approved version.

Base on-screen warning (short-form video)

Generic base — keep on-screen, legible, full duration "Trading CFDs and forex carries a high risk of loss and may not be suitable for everyone. A high proportion of retail accounts lose money trading these products. You could lose your entire deposit. This is general information, not investment advice. See the full risk disclosure at valetax.com."

Note on the wording: This generic line deliberately avoids a specific loss figure. In the UK/EU it must be replaced with the firm-specific standardised warning below; never carry a vague "most accounts lose money" claim into those markets. And only state that clients could lose more than their deposit where Valetax genuinely offers no negative-balance protection [VERIFY] — in markets where such protection is mandatory, that claim is itself inaccurate.

The region template from your audit (Singapore example)

Per-region — adapt the regulator line for each market "This content serves as general information and does not constitute an investment recommendation. The risk of loss in leveraged trading can be substantial. Trading CFDs may not be suitable for all individuals. Losses can exceed your deposit. Refer to the risk fact sheet and risk disclosure statement at valetax.com. This advertisement has not been reviewed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore."
Important on the "not reviewed by MAS" line That sentence is the format used by MAS-licensed or exempt entities. It does not, by itself, make soliciting Singapore residents lawful for an unlicensed broker — and every market needs its own wording (in-market language, the correct regulator, and the locally-mandated risk statement). [VERIFY] each region's exact required text with local counsel before use.

Placement rules

  • On-screen, not caption-only. Caption / "see more…" / end-card-only placement fails review in strict markets.
  • Legible & persistent. Readable size and contrast, held clearly long enough to read in full — on a short clip that usually means keeping it persistent rather than flashing it (treat roughly five seconds, or the whole video if shorter, as a practical floor [VERIFY]). Repeat it on any frame that shows a benefit.
  • Equal prominence. The risk text shouldn't be visibly smaller or dimmer than the benefit message.
  • Mirror it on the landing page — full risk disclosure + T&Cs, matching the ad.

UK/EU note (if those markets are ever in scope) conditional

UK/EU mandate a standardised CFD warning with a firm-specific loss percentage ("XX% of retail investor accounts lose money when trading CFDs with this provider…"), recalculated quarterly. Do not invent the number; it must come from Valetax's actual client data and counsel. The generic base above is not a substitute there. Australia (ASIC) and Singapore (MAS) do not use this firm-specific percentage warning — it is a UK/EU requirement only, so don't carry it into AU/SG creative.

Section 06 · The platform

06  TikTok's own rules

Separate from any country's law, TikTok runs its own gate — and for a trading broker it's strict. Meet these requirements, or the content never reaches an audience.

Two rulebooks, both apply
  • Paid Ads policy — treats forex/CFD as a licensed, market-by-market category: you must run from a verified, category-whitelisted advertiser account, hold local licensing, and age-gate to the required minimum (18+, but confirm the minimum and whether the category is eligible at all in that market [VERIFY]).
  • Branded Content policy — bans paid-creator / partnership-toggle posts for forex, CFDs, and crypto everywhere. The paid-creator route that many brokers rely on is closed.
Authorisation before spend
  • This category isn't self-serve. Expect to apply through a TikTok sales rep / account manager, submit licensing + registration evidence, and get the account whitelisted first.
  • [VERIFY] the exact document checklist and turnaround with the rep — it isn't published. This is the licensing-and-docs task already on the Toggle ⇄ Valetax tracker.
The destination is reviewed too
  • TikTok scans the landing page, not just the video: offer must match, T&Cs and risk disclosure present, no unlicensed or prohibited offers.
  • Never change the landing page after approval — it is read as evading review and results in account suspension.
Account health & appeals
  • Violations escalate: rejection → restriction → suspension. Accumulated rejections degrade account standing, so treat account health as a finite, protected resource.
  • Appeals run through Ads Manager; do not file repeat appeals for the same issue, and do not open new accounts while one is pending.

Paid vs organic — what changes

Valetax will also post to its own organic feed. The mechanics differ, but the content rules in Sections 2–5 apply to both.

Paid ads

  • Needs the verified, whitelisted advertiser account, licensing, age-gate, and landing-page review.
  • Goes through ad review before it can serve — rejection is visible and appealable.

Organic posts

  • No whitelisting or landing-page mechanics — but Community Guidelines and the same finance-content scrutiny still apply, and the Branded-Content ban still blocks paid-creator trading posts.
  • Enforcement is quieter here (takedowns, reach limits, account strikes), so the disclaimer and red-flag rules matter just as much.

Section 08 · Distribution

08  Targeting & language

From the campaign-setup half of the audit — once content is compliant and the account is cleared, these settings sharpen who sees it and reduce the chance of confused-viewer reports.

Refine to financial intent

Layer interest and behaviour targeting toward Financial Services, Finance apps, Business & Finance, Business & Productivity. Narrowing toward finance and business audiences improves audience quality and lowers the chance of irrelevant viewers reporting the ad as inappropriate or confusing.

Language by market

Match language to market — Vietnamese for VN, Thai for TH, etc. For a wider reach you may add English + local. Caveat from Section 7: in TH, native-language targeting of residents is exactly the high-risk pattern — coordinate language choices with the market/legal view, not just performance.

Keep the structure, fix the creative

The campaign structure itself is broadly fine; the blockers are creative and compliance, not architecture. Do not over-engineer the setup; direct the effort toward content that clears review and toward 18+ age-gating.
