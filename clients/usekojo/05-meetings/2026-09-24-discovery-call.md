## Why they are talking to us

Alex joined Kojo a little over three months ago and leads a team of three: a product marketer, an events marketer and a demand gen marketer. The demand gen marketer comes from a pure paid background and does not think about demand gen holistically, so he leaves on 5 October. Alex described the paid program as "a lot of just kind of ongoing maintenance", with slow campaign launches and room to optimize. He wants someone to manage spend and add structure and strategy. The long-term plan is to bring the role back in-house as a generalist growth marketer who can also open new channels. Hiring will take about three months.

## Funnel and sales

- Journey: ad, website, a high-intent CTA (request a demo, request pricing, contact), then sales.
- Chili Piper lets the lead book directly on an account executive's calendar. A lead who fills the form without booking goes to the SDR team. Kojo is adding a rule that routes no-shows back to SDRs.
- There is no free trial, no product-led motion and no lead scoring.
- After the demo comes a three to five step evaluation, because the product is technical and every contractor has its own suppliers.
- **Buying committee:** at mid-market, a leadership role up to the president or CEO. At enterprise, procurement, operations and field managers, with finance as a lower stakeholder. Managers are influencers, not decision-makers.
- **ACV:** about $30,000 a year mid-market and about $70,000 enterprise.
- **Cycle:** some deals close in 60 to 90 days and larger ones take longer. Kojo's own average is about 90 days.
- **Win rate:** Alex said "roughly 20%" while doing CPL math out loud, and did not finish the calculation. Jordan asked him to email it.

## What we see in the numbers (our analysis, not client statements)

1. **Lead volume is small.** At $80,000 a month and a cost per lead over $1,000, Kojo gets at most about 80 leads a month. At $50,000 and the same cost per lead, that falls to about 50. Every test has to be read against this volume, so weekly reads will be noisy.
2. **A $1,000 cost per lead may be affordable.** If about 16% of leads close (Alex's 20% win rate after roughly 80% of leads become opportunities, which is one reading of his unfinished math), one $30,000 customer costs about $6,250 in media. That is well under first-year ACV. If the 20% is opportunity to close and fewer leads become opportunities, the picture changes. The proposal should frame the goal as cost per qualified opportunity and pipeline per dollar, and show the lead-to-opportunity rate as the input we need.
3. **Search is the proven engine.** The July and August accident links search spend to lead volume. Cutting $30,000 should come mostly from Meta, not search.
4. **Meta targets the wrong person for a $25M+ account-based motion.** Meta cannot target by company revenue or job title the way LinkedIn can. Unless it runs on uploaded account or contact lists, much of the $30,000 likely reaches people outside the ICP. We confirm what we can from the Meta Ad Library.
5. **Attribution comes before optimization.** With source capture failing on some leads, no channel decision can be read with confidence. Hidden UTM and click ID fields on the form, stored through the Chili Piper hop, plus a "how did you hear about us" field, are the week 1 fixes.
