A programmatic vendor has approached us, and the channel keeps coming up. Before committing real budget, the honest question is simple: can programmatic deliver UNITAR leads at a cost that competes with Meta and Google — or not? This deck lays out both sides and a cheap way to find the real answer.

Programmatic, In Plain Language

Programmatic is automated ad-buying across the open internet — news sites, apps, streaming, banners and video — through an auction, in milliseconds. Meta and Google are closed gardens; programmatic is the open web outside them.

What it's genuinely good at
  • Reach. Puts UNITAR in front of students across thousands of sites and apps Meta/Google ads never appear on.
  • Awareness at scale. Cheap impressions to warm up a market before they ever search — useful for new programs and new campuses.
  • Retargeting across the web. Follow a known visitor onto other sites (we treat this separately — see the retargeting deck).
The honest limitation
  • It targets by guesswork. Without logged-in data, it infers who you are from browsing signals — less precise than Meta/Google.
  • Fees stack. The ad-buying platform, the data, and the vendor each take a cut before a single student sees the ad.
  • Lower intent. A banner impression is a long way from a student filling a lead form. The funnel is longer.
Bottom line: strong for reach, unproven for cost-per-lead. The test below is built to find out which one wins for UNITAR.

The Test — Three Gates, RM 30k

RM 30k total

Step 0 (free): get the vendor's pay-per-lead price first. If it's plainly competitive, that alone may answer the question. If we still want our own read, we walk down the funnel one phase at a time — each must clear its gate before we release the next RM 10k. If a gate fails, we stop.

01RM 10k
Goal: Impressions
Can we buy quality, brand-safe space in front of the right Malaysian students at a sane cost per thousand views?
Gate → ads actually seen by real students, at a sane cost, on safe sites
02RM 10k
Goal: Clicks
Does that audience actually engage — click through and behave like real prospects on the landing page?
Gate → click rate & cost per click beat our usual display benchmark
03RM 10k
Goal: Cost per lead
The real question: leads at a cost that competes with Meta/Google — and that go on to validate in Salesforce.
Gate → cost per lead within ~20% of our ~RM99 benchmark, early validation holding
Spend is sequential, never all-in. The most we can ever lose is the phase we're in — and we only enter a phase the last one earned.
Slide 6 · Risks & Guardrails — Belief: "They named the risks before I had to."

What Could Go Wrong — and How We Cap It

Every concern our team raised, on the table, each with a guardrail. None of these are reasons not to test; they're reasons to test the cheap way.

Margin / stacked fees
DSP + data + vendor fees eat into media. Guardrail: fixed RM30k cap, full fee transparency up front, and the ex-WPP inventory rates working in our favour.
Inference vs. real data
Targeting is modelled, not logged-in. Guardrail: Phases 1–2 test whether the inference is good enough before a single CPL ringgit is spent.
Measurement & attribution
We can't judge lead quality unless it traces into Salesforce. Guardrail: lead-source tagging confirmed with Advertica before launch — the same mapping already underway for Pakistan/Singapore. And since some leads only validate weeks later, Phase 3 gives an early read, not a final verdict.
Brand safety
Open-web inventory can sit beside the wrong content. Guardrail: publisher allow-list + category exclusions signed off by UNITAR before any impression runs.
The honest downside: programmatic may simply lose to Meta/Google on CPL. If it does, we'll have proof for RM30k — and we walk away knowing, not guessing.
