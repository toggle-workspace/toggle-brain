We're Pouring Leads Into a Leaky Bucket

Lead volume is healthy — up 15% week on week. The problem isn't the top of the funnel; it's what happens after. Fewer of those leads are surviving to an appointment than last year, and the gap is widening as we scale.

Why pouring in more leads won't fix it
Every leaked lead was already paid for — the ad spend, the click, the form fill all happened. When half go cold, more prospecting just pours more water into the same leaky bucket. The cheapest lead UNITAR can buy is the warm one it already has. Part of the gap may be sales capacity or uncontactable leads — root cause is still being confirmed with Shifa; retargeting recovers the reachable slice first, while that's investigated.
This isn't a lead-volume problem. It's a re-engagement problem — and it has its own fix.
Slide 3 · Why It's Separate — Belief: "This won't cannibalise my lead-gen volume."

A Separate Layer — Not a Cut of Lead-Gen

Retargeting does a different job, on a different audience, measured a different way. It must sit on its own budget so it never competes with — or quietly drains — the prospecting that fills the top of the funnel.

Primary lead-gen (today)Retargeting layer (new)
JobFind brand-new students — fill the top of the funnelRe-engage warm leads who didn't convert — seal the bottom
AudienceCold — people who've never heard from UNITARWarm — already clicked, watched, or half-filled a form
Cost to reachFull price — you're paying to be discoveredA fraction — you already paid to acquire them once
ScoreboardCPL — cost per new leadCost per recovered validated lead — a different KPI
BudgetThe core media plan (RM-millions/year)New, incremental, ring-fenced — never drawn from the above
The reason this matters: if retargeting shared the lead-gen budget, every ringgit spent re-engaging a warm lead would be a ringgit not spent finding a new one — and volume targets would quietly slip. Separate budgets keep both jobs honest.
Slide 4 · Who We Re-Engage — Belief: "There's real warm demand sitting unused."

Four Warm Audiences You've Already Paid For

These students are already inside UNITAR's world — they raised a hand and then stalled. Each group needs a different nudge, matched to exactly where they dropped off.

Hottest
Form abandoners
Opened a lead form, started filling, never submitted. The single warmest audience there is — they were one tap from converting.
Stalled
Validating-stage leads gone cold
Submitted, entered the validating stage, then went quiet — the exact cohort behind the ~50% gap. We re-surface them with a reason to re-engage.
Engaged

Browsed programs or campuses but never enquired. Known visitors we can follow back with the specific program they looked at.

All four already exist in your pixels and CRM. Retargeting just stops letting them go to waste.
Slide 5 · How It Works — Belief: "There's a real mechanism here, not just 'show more ads.'"

How the Layer Works

Not "chase them with the same ad." A sequenced, intent-aware nudge that meets each student where they stalled — and feeds the result straight back into the ad engines so they learn what actually validates.

01
Match to the drop-off
A different message for a form-abandoner vs. a stalled validating lead. Relevance is the whole game.
→
02
Sequence the nudge
2–3 touches over days, not one blast — a deadline reminder, a campus proof point, an easy re-entry.
→
03
Higher-intent re-entry
Send them to a short, phone-verified form (a one-time code) so the recovered lead is actually contactable — not another dead end.
→
04
Feed the signal back
Our tracking now follows leads to the "applied" stage, so we can push the ads toward students who actually validate — not just submit.
We tag it separately. Retargeting carries its own labels in Salesforce, so we can prove the recovered leads are genuinely extra — not prospecting leads we'd have won anyway.
Pairs with the nurturing team. Ads re-open the door; the nurturing team walks them through it. Same goal — shorten time-to-close — from two sides.
Slide 6 · Budget & Measurement — Belief: "I know what it costs, how it's judged, and that the number's still open."

Budget & Measurement

PROPOSED · figure to confirm

A small, incremental starter layer — sized to prove the economics before scaling. Because the audience is warm and already acquired, the cost to recover a lead should sit well below the cost to find a brand-new one.

Incremental starter budget
RM 12k / mo
A new line item, on top of — never carved out of — the core lead-gen plan. We'd start here and scale only on a proven cost-per-recovered-lead — final number set with you.
A different scoreboard
  • Primary KPI: cost per recovered, validated lead — not CPL
  • Defined tightly: a lead that validates after a retargeting touch and wasn't already being worked by sales/nurturing — so we never claim their wins
  • Why not CPL: these leads were counted once already; CPL would double-count and flatter the numbers
  • Success: recovered leads cost less per validated student than net-new prospecting
Why the budget can be small: warm audiences are a fraction of the size of cold ones — you don't need millions in spend to reach everyone who already raised a hand. A modest, well-aimed layer covers the whole pool. Optimising all the way to validation can raise cost-per-action (the team's own estimate: up to ~3×) — but on a warm, already-acquired audience the absolute spend stays small.
