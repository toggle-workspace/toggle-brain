Internal planning document. The client deck built from this ships white-labeled
under Audaura, with no Toggle or Madcrack mark anywhere in it.

**Window: 1 November 2026 to 28 February 2027.** This is the C1 2027 cycle, not a
calendar quarter. UNITAR budgets in cycles, and the precedent on file is
"C1 2026 (Nov25 to Feb26): RM3,200,000 net."

Monthly actuals from `01-strategy/account-knowledge-base.md` §9.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Two comparisons carry the pitch.

**C1 2026 as a whole against Q1 2026.** The four C1 months delivered 26,599 leads
on RM2,424,409, a blended RM91.15. The three Q1 months delivered 17,442 leads on
RM1,960,428, a blended RM112.40. Same account, same team, 19% cheaper leads.

**The front half against the back half.** November and December together ran
RM78.53. January and February together ran RM106.96. The cheap money in this
cycle sits in the first eight weeks.

### The finding that should open the deck

On the week of 27 October 2025 the reporting doc records the plan for the cycle:
scale to roughly RM500,000 in November "while backloading to save budget for end
of cycle 1."

**C1 2026 was deliberately backloaded into its most expensive weeks.** The budget
was held back for January and February, and January and February delivered leads
at RM106.96 against RM78.53 in November and December.

The obvious objection is that November looked cheap because it spent less, so the
comparison is really a scale effect. Check it at matched spend and it holds.
December ran RM761,459 and January ran RM739,793, within 3% of each other, and
December returned RM86.40 against January's RM95.60. February then produced the
worst CPL on record at RM127.50 on the *lowest* spend of the four months. The
back half is more expensive at every spend level tested.

Front-loading C1 2027 is the single highest-value recommendation in this plan, it
costs nothing to implement, and it is provable from UNITAR's own reporting.

Two caveats to state honestly in the room, because Tim will check. December 2025
carried the website hack, and February 2026 carried the reCAPTCHA bug plus roughly
a week of paused Google spend. Both confounds sit inside the window. They cut in
opposite directions and the pattern survives either way, but present them rather
than waiting to be asked.

## 2. The cycle map

C1 2027 runs November 2026 through February 2027, closing on the January intake.
The cycle character in the knowledge base is "second largest, postgrad plus online
plus working adults."

| Phase | Dates | Job |

working adults, postgrad candidates, online learners and career changers, which is

That points the program mix at the online MBA (RM12K at UUCKL, RM19K at UIU, and
the entity distinction is a compliance requirement in the ad itself), PGDE, the
four Master of Education specialisms, APEL entry routes, and the Perkeso EIS line.
Early Childhood Education stays the volume engine, since it is the number one
demand program in every cycle.

## 4. What the same window taught us last year

### The best week on record in this cycle, 10 to 16 November 2025

27% from 309 to 393. The reporting doc calls it "the strongest weekly performance
in November." What drove it:

|---|---|---|
| FB / BOF / Lead Gen / Instant Form / A2 / T1a+T1b / ABO | 75 | **33.43** |

| FB / BOF / Lead Gen / Instant Form / A1 / T1a / ABO / CAPI | 130 | 54.13 |
| Google Search, all campaigns that week | 519 | 57.00 |

The cheapest line was **A2, which is BOF retargeting**, at RM33.43. Retargeting was
the single most efficient thing in the account during the November window, and the
retargeting workstream is currently approved and blocked on Ron's pool extract.

CAPI also proved itself here: T1a CAPI improved 16% week on week from RM64.17 to
RM54.13, which the doc notes as "validating the 2-3 week learning period
projection." Read that as a deadline. **CAPI needs two to three weeks of learning
before the cheap window opens**, which means the structure has to be live by late
October to be earning by 9 November.

Google Search at RM57 in mid-November is also worth noting, because Google gets
expensive later in the same cycle.

### The three failures that cost money, all avoidable

1. **Creative specs.** "New creatives launched in December were limited in terms of
   sizes and dimensions, causing us to narrow down placements we can show ads on,
   leading to an increase in CPL." A production error, not a market condition.
2. **Landing pages, again.** December Google CPA rose 60% from RM72 to RM117, and
   the doc attributes it to "ads being temporarily disabled after Google crawlers
   detected inactive landing pages." This is the fourth instance of the account's
   highest-frequency catastrophe.
3. **Year-end auction pressure.** The doc names "end of year sale season" as a
   December cost driver, alongside "year end seasonality has shown a drop in search
   volume for the education vertical particularly, and increase in CPL in general
   for Google SEM." It then clears: "Year end seasonality period has passed and CPL
   is stabilizing again."

That last one is a media-mix instruction, not a warning. **Weight Meta through
late December and bring Google back in January.** Meta posted its best-ever RM55
in November while Google search volume was falling.

### The Open Day, which is an unclaimed opportunity

UNITAR ran an Open Day from 8 to 13 December 2025. Meta support was RM4,543.59 for
1,057,279 reach, 5,353 clicks, 71,755 page engagements and **9 direct leads.**

The recorded learning was that "boosting interactive social media posts are best to
generate engagement related performance at low costs" and that "more straight to
the point, explanatory static ads drive more traffic and direct leads."

A million people reached and nine leads captured. The Open Day is currently run as
an awareness post rather than as a registration funnel. Registration, reminder,
attendance and follow-up is precisely what a WhatsApp automation layer does, and it
is the cleanest demonstration of the respond.io value in the entire pitch.

## 5. The strategic thesis

UNITAR does not have a lead volume problem in C1. It has a conversion, timing and
retention problem, and the account file proves it five ways.

1. **Only about 50% of leads validate through to an appointment, against roughly
   71% across the prior full year.** Twenty-one points of warm demand leaking, root
   cause still unidentified, open with Shifa since late June 2026.
2. **Around 29,000 leads created in 2026 against 52,800 since 2019, with about 47%
   carrying over each cycle.** The validated extract is still pending from Ron.
3. **91% of Meta form-fillers then click through to WhatsApp.** The form is the
   handoff point, and the handoff is manual.
4. **Ideal first response time is 24 hours** in the Salesforce funnel definition.
5. **Pakistan produced 200 leads in five days at RM11 CPL, then hit 82%
   uncontactable on WhatsApp.** Non-response, not fake numbers.

None of that is fixed by buying more media. That is the wedge for expanding scope.

Layer the seasonal finding on top. The reporting doc documents the same pattern
across every festive period: on CNY, "softer conversion intent... impacting CVR
despite stable engagement metrics"; on Ramadan, "users are more likely to browse
but delay decision-making, resulting in lower CVR despite consistent engagement";
and afterward, "user intent normalized after the festive season, supporting
stronger conversion performance."

**Engagement holds and conversion rate drops. Intent defers rather than
disappearing.** In C1 2027 that matters twice, at the year-end trough and again
across the CNY and Ramadan collision in February. Without a nurture layer, leads
bought cheaply in November and December decay before the January intake closes.

**The one-line pitch: buy the cycle's demand in its cheapest eight weeks, hold it
through the festive windows, and convert it at the January intake instead of
paying full price for it again in March.**

### The metric to move

The KPI stays net lead gap per campus, and blended CPL remains an explicit
anti-goal. The offer should add **cost per validated lead**, since that is where
the 21-point leak shows up and it is the number a CRM layer directly moves. The
RFP already carries CPA under RM1,000 and 10% lead-to-enrolment as stated targets,
so this is an unmet idea rather than a new one.

Use the 2024 reconciliation to size the prize, never as a live range: platform CPL
of about RM95 against Salesforce-attributed cost per outcome of RM926.80, on two
different attribution bases, two years old.

Ten campus pages plus a monitoring layer is contained, provable, and moves the

### Tier 3, the fuel: creative production at festive cadence

C1 2027 needs four creative waves against a two-week refresh cadence that the

Include a hard creative-spec gate in the scope, given December 2025.

### Tier 4, the long game: SEO and GEO

Toggle promised UNITAR a keyword visibility report for ChatGPT and Gemini in the

### Tier 5, the proof layer: reporting

The Meta ads dashboard claims live data in three places and is not live. The

## 8. Campaigns A and B, college and degree seekers

The majority of the spend and the whole of the per-campus KPI.

### Who is in market from November to February

- **SPM 2025 leavers who did not enroll** in the March, May or September 2026
  intakes. Their results came out 31 March 2026, so by January 2027 they are ten
  months out and January is their next real chance.
- **Foundation and diploma completers** progressing to the next level, which is
  what the foundation-to-degree pathway sells.
- **Students switching** after an unhappy first semester somewhere else.
- **Working adults** going after a first degree part-time or online.
- **The parents**, who co-decide and usually pay.

> ⚠️ **Who is NOT in market: the SPM 2026 cohort.** They sit written papers across
> November and December 2026 and do not get results until around end of March
> 2027. They are the April buyer, not the January buyer. Do not build C1 around
> them, and do not let anyone assume post-SPM messaging works in this window.

### What moves them, from the approved C1 copy bank

- **Affordability.** PTPTN up to 100%, scholarships up to 100%, free accommodation
  at selected locations. B40 audience, so cost is the first barrier, not the third.
- **Proximity.** Ten campuses, study near home, keep living costs down. The
  existing line is "kualiti KL tanpa kos hidup KL."
- **Speed to work.** Diploma in 2.5 years, foundation in 1 year.
- **Employability.** 98% graduate employability, 100+ industry partners,
  Employer's Preferred University from Talentbank.
- **Pathway certainty.** Foundation into the degree at the same campus, no
  reapplying, credits carry.

### Mechanics

| # | Mechanic | How it runs | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **U1** | Cost-after-PTPTN check | A "what will this actually cost me" calculator or eligibility check as the lead magnet, rather than a generic enquiry form | Cost is the first barrier for a B40 audience. Answering it up front self-selects intent and gives the counsellor a warm opener |
| **U2** | The family decision pack | A shareable one-pager the student sends their parents: cost after PTPTN, campus location, employability, career outcomes. Delivered on WhatsApp through respond.io | Targets the real decision unit. The student is the lead, the parents are the buyer, and nothing we produce currently speaks to the parent |
| U3 | Campus-named creative | Per-campus creative naming the city, one base times ten campuses times program | The C2 strategy calls making the campus location immediately visible the single highest-impact creative element. Still not consistently done |
| U4 | Pathway guarantee | Foundation into degree at the same campus, no reapplication, credits carry, stated as a guarantee rather than a feature | Removes the "will I have to move or reapply again" fear. Already in the copy bank but buried as a bullet |
| U5 | Free accommodation callout | Lead with it in outstation creative rather than listing it fifth | Already offered and badly under-used. For a B40 family, accommodation is a bigger number than tuition after PTPTN |
| U6 | Reactivate the cold pool at festive moments | Work the 29,000-lead pool during Deepavali and CNY specifically, not evenly across the cycle | Most of that pool is undergrad leads that went cold. Festive family pressure is when they become reachable again |
| U7 | Open Day, family track | Rebuild the December Open Day as a registration funnel that explicitly invites parents, respond.io handling registration, reminders and follow-up | Nine leads from a million reach today. Bringing the parent to campus is the highest-converting thing UNITAR can do and it is unmeasured |
| U8 | Gold at the family festivals | The prize gated at application, running at Deepavali and CNY. See §10 and §11 | Gives UNITAR the large-scale gift they like, at the moment the family is together, without repeating the Myvi mistake |

## 9. Campaign C, postgrad and working adults

One campaign, not the strategy. Highest margin, smallest volume, and it owns the
year-end beat because the money levers only work for someone with a job.

| Lever | What it is | Why it lands here |
|---|---|---|

**The line this campaign hangs on:** a working adult who pays before 31 December
can fund it from EPF and claim the relief in this year's assessment. Miss the date
and the same decision costs them a full year of waiting.

The buyer, from the MBA creator brief: Malaysian working professionals aged 26 to
40, already holding a bachelor's degree, "feeling stuck at their current level,"
unwilling to quit their job or move cities. Programs are MBA Online (RM12,000 at
UUCKL, RM19,000 at UIU, and ads must state which entity), the five MBA
specialisms, MBA APEL Q, PGDE, the four Master of Education specialisms, and the
doctorates.

| # | Mechanic | How it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | The 31 December clock | Countdown from mid-November. The deadline is the payment date, not the application date, with a fallback intake message for anyone who misses it |
| **P2** | EPF funding path | A guided explainer taking someone from "no spare cash" to a submitted KWSP withdrawal, delivered on WhatsApp through respond.io |

## 10. Large-scale gift hooks, what the record actually says

UNITAR likes big prizes and we have run three. Lined up side by side the pattern
is clear.

| Promotion | When | Gated at | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold, 20g | May to Jun 2025, C2 | **Enrolment** | Ran under the Mindshare framework with its own landing page at `unitar.my/m/c/c2-2025-gold`. Later cited as a driver of higher website traffic in Apr to Jun 2025. No CPL or enrolment performance on file |
| Myvi car | Jul to Sep 2025, C3 | **Lead form** | Launched for the September intake. Early: "running with CPL much lower than the account average." Then it turned. Cut 20%, "CPL decreased by 27%." Still "driving up overall CPL," cut again, "CPL for the car giveaway campaign decreased by 48%." **Ended paused** |
| **Fee waiver** | Late Jan 2026, C1 | **Fee, tied to the product** | "Strong improvements in engagement and conversion performance, higher CTR and improved CVR." T1a conversions **up 62.79%** with stable CTR. The read: the incentive "effectively increased intent rather than just click volume." By week two it had started "increasing curiosity-driven clicks" and diluting CVR |
| MacBook Neo | Pending | WhatsApp message | Agreed CTA for the retargeting pool, about RM5k a month across four ad sets. Not launched, blocked on Ron's extract. Foundation cohort eligibility open |

**Rule 1. The further down the funnel you gate the prize, the better it
performs.** A prize gated at the lead form buys everyone who wants a car. A prize
gated at application or enrolment buys applicants. The gold campaign was an
enrolment incentive. The Myvi was a lead-gen campaign.

**Rule 2. Prize relevance filters intent.** A car has nothing to do with
studying. A fee waiver is the product getting cheaper. Gold at a festival is a
gift the family recognizes. The closer the prize sits to the moment and the buyer,
the less junk it pulls in.

**If UNITAR wants a headline gift for C1:** gate it at application or enrolment,
put it on Campaigns A and B where the family is the decision unit and a gift
carries social weight rather than on the postgrad campaign where the buyer is
running a spreadsheet, ring-fence it as its own campaign with separate reporting,
and give it a hard end date. The Myvi's real damage was sitting inside blended CPL
and dragging the account number with it.

## 11. Festive periods, which campaign each serves, the mechanic, and why

> **Why campaign during a festive period at all.** The account's reporting says the
> same thing about every festival: engagement holds and conversion rate drops. So
> the reason to be there is not to convert on the day. For Campaigns A and B it is
> because the family is the decision unit and festivals are when the family is
> physically together. An 18-year-old does not choose a college alone, they choose
> it over a meal with their parents while relatives ask what the plan is. That
> conversation is the product we are advertising into.

### Beat 1. Deepavali, Sunday 8 November

**Serves Campaigns A and B, college and degree.** All states except Sarawak,
replacement Monday 9 November. Opens the cycle.

**Mechanic: gold, gated at application, plus the family decision pack (U2).**
Buying gold around Deepavali is common practice, so the 20g gold prize UNITAR
already ran finally gets a cultural anchor. It ran in May and June 2025, which is
not a gold-buying season for anyone. Same prize, same production cost, far more
relevance. Run PTPTN and free-accommodation messaging alongside it, and work the
cold pool (U6).

Why a campaign belongs here:

- It opens the cycle immediately before the cheapest week on record. 10 to 16
  November 2025 delivered RM41.91 blended.
- Families gather, and "what are you doing next year" gets asked out loud. For a
  17 to 22 year old that is the decision moment.
- Festive advances and bonuses are commonly paid ahead of major festivals, so the
  parents who pay have cash in hand.
- People travel to hometowns, which is where the outstation campuses carrying the
  per-campus gap actually are.

**Risk:** the "~70% Indian students" figure is unverified, and the gold-buying
custom is stronger in some communities than others, so sanity-check both with
UNITAR before either becomes a pillar. This also sits in the week we must not
disrupt, so the festive wave runs alongside the core engine, never in place of it.

### Beat 2. Year-end and New Year, 25 December to 1 January

**Serves Campaign C, postgrad, with a secondary line for degree.** The one beat
where postgrad leads.

**Mechanic: the 31 December tax deadline, plus a fee waiver wave.** No prize. The
offer is the deadline itself and the waiver is the accelerant, run as a short
flight with a hard end date. For Campaigns A and B the same window carries a
"start the year enrolled" message against the January intake deadline, but without
the tax mechanic, which does not apply to a school leaver with no taxable income.

Why a campaign belongs here:

- Malaysia's year of assessment closes 31 December, so paying by then moves the
  RM7,000 relief a full year earlier. An external deadline we did not invent.
- Year-end bonuses are already paid.
- Annual reflection is real behavior, and it is on-message for a buyer "feeling
  stuck at their current level."
- The January intake needs the decision now regardless of any promotion.

**Risk:** the account documents a year-end drop in education search volume, rising
Google SEM CPL, and "end of year sale season" auction pressure. Meta-weighted and
message-led, not a Google push. Google returns in January.

### Beat 3. Chinese New Year, 6 to 7 February, with Awal Ramadan on 8 February

**Serves Campaigns A and B, and seeds C2 rather than closing C1.** It falls after
the January intake closes. Include it on that basis or not at all.

**Mechanic: an angpow-framed fee rebate, gated at application, plus Mandarin
creative.** A rebate presented as an angpow at a culturally resonant figure. Gold
carries here too, since gold buying also spikes at CNY, which means one prize pool
can serve both Beat 1 and Beat 3. The Mandarin creative addresses a named RM300k
problem area where ads are not running and existing translations are poor.

Why a campaign belongs here:

- The reunion dinner is the most concentrated family-decision moment in the
  Malaysian Chinese calendar, and the college question gets asked at the table.
- CNY bonuses are paid before the festival.
- Attention is high even though conversion is low, so it is cheap to be remembered.
- Chap Goh Meh on 20 February gives a quieter second beat once the crowd clears.

**Risk:** two major festivals inside 72 hours make this the lowest-CVR window of
the cycle. Shift objective rather than cutting budget: move to reach and
conversation from 6 February, at RM13 to RM31 per conversation, and let respond.io
hold the leads into C2.

### Beat 4. Thaipusam, Friday 22 January

Dropped by Zaid. Listed only so the calendar is complete.

### One prize pool, two festivals

Gold is culturally loaded for both the Deepavali audience in November and the
Chinese New Year audience in February. A single pool procured once anchors both
beats, which makes the gold mechanic cheaper per beat than it looks and gives
UNITAR the large-scale gift they like without a second procurement cycle.

### What not to write

Straight from the compliance record. These are not style preferences.

- Never state the RM12,000 MBA without naming UUCKL, because UIU's is RM19,000.
- Never frame EPF withdrawal as free money. No salary, promotion or guaranteed-job
  claims. No named competitor comparisons in creative.
- Special-needs B.Ed may only use the phrase "Slow Learner." Foundation in Arts
  PTPTN is UIU only, not UUCKL. The DOSH diploma is PTPTN-loanable but not
  DOSH-department accredited.
- Banned from the MBA brief: "invest in yourself," "CEO mindset," "boss life,"
  "the world is changing fast," and flashing luxury cars, watches or cash.
- Approved CTAs are "Learn More" and "Apply Now." Live social copy uses "Chat with
  our counsellors," which is softer and performs.

### Format notes

- Native and creator-style beats high production by roughly twenty times on TikTok
  Instant Form submission rate, 0.62% against 0.03%.
- Ads carrying pricing were top performers through a full month.
- Hyper-specific program naming wins, generic loses. `generic_certificate` was a
  bottom performer, and so was anything too niche.
- BM is mandatory in outstation states. English only misses 50 to 70% of search
  volume there.

> ⚠️ **Claims to verify before any of this runs.** "Malaysia is short of teachers"
> appears in the approved copy bank as fact with no source in the repo. So does
> "the only private university with PTPTN up to 100% for Foundation," which is a
> strong exclusivity claim. Re-confirm the 98% employability figure, the 100+
> industry partners, the Employer's Preferred University award, and the
> free-accommodation terms per campus for C1 2027.

## 13. Borrowed from outside education

Every university in Malaysia runs the same playbook: open day, scholarship post,
convocation photo, repeat. The brands worth stealing from are not universities.
Grouped by how much it costs to say yes.

### Big swings, needing budget and a client champion

**1. Campus Loop, a run club at all ten campuses.** Serves Campaign A. Borrowed
from **Nike Run Club**, **Adidas Runners** and **Strava**. A free 5K loop every
Saturday that starts and ends at the campus. No enrolment required, sign-up
through WhatsApp so respond.io captures a number, a town and consent.

The reason it is not a gimmick: outstation campuses have a familiarity problem,
not only a media problem. A 19-year-old in Sungai Petani has never physically been
on that campus and cannot picture themselves there. A run club puts them on it
fifty times a year before they ever see an ad, and it feeds the per-campus KPI
directly.

- Strava layer: register all ten campuses as clubs, run a monthly leaderboard
  between them. Inter-campus rivalry is free content and mirrors our reporting.
- Finish-line partner: a local kopitiam or **ZUS Coffee** near each campus.
- Line: "You will be on campus every Saturday. Enrolling is optional."
  BM: "Setiap Sabtu anda dah ada di kampus. Daftar tu pilihan."

> ⚠️ **Raise the objection before UNITAR does.** Run clubs skew middle class and
> the undergrad target market is B40. In Kota Bharu or Sungai Petani, running shoes
> and a free Saturday morning are not universal. Pilot at two campuses, not ten,
> and read the turnout honestly. Idea 7 is the version without this problem.

**2. Study Hall, the campus as a third place.** Serves all three campaigns.
Borrowed from **Reading Rhythms**, **Silent Book Clubs**, **WeWork** and every
**ZUS Coffee** in the country. Open the campus evenings and weekends as free study
space. Wifi, aircon, coffee, long tables. Student or not.

Malaysian students already study at mamak stalls, McDonald's and ZUS because home
is crowded. They are paying for a seat. UNITAR has ten buildings sitting empty
every evening. Marginal cost close to zero, and it gives the working-adult
postgrad audience somewhere to study after work.

- Probably a better first bet than the run club: cheaper, on-brand, no fitness
  barrier, works in the rain.
- Line: "The library is open. You do not have to be a student."
  BM: "Perpustakaan dah buka. Tak perlu jadi pelajar pun boleh masuk."

**3. 12.12 Intake Day.** Serves all three campaigns. Borrowed from **Shopee 11.11**
and **Lazada 12.12**. Shopee and Lazada spent a decade training every Malaysian
that a double date means act now. That conditioning is free, deeply set, and
nobody in education uses it.

Run 12 December as a single-day intake event: application fee waived, gold draw
closes, counsellors staffed all day. An artificial deadline the market already
knows how to respond to, landing inside the peak efficiency window days after the
Open Day.

- Commercially this may be the most useful idea here, because it converts UNITAR's
  appetite for promotions into a mechanic the audience is already trained for.
- Line: "12.12 is usually for things you throw away in a year. This one lasts."

**4. Your Year, Wrapped.** Serves Campaign C. Borrowed from **Spotify Wrapped**,
**Strava Year in Sport** and **Duolingo Year in Review**. December is when the
internet posts a summary of its year. Wrapped works because it is a mirror, and
for a lot of people the reflection is uncomfortable.

A shareable career version: a few inputs, then a card reading something like "You
have held the same job title for 1,095 days." Ends on the January intake and the
31 December payment deadline. It is a landing page, so it sits inside the CRO tier
we are already pitching.

- Line: "Your Wrapped tells you what you listened to. This one tells you what you
  did."

### Creative territories, no new budget

**5. Loud about the cost.** Serves Campaign A and the parent-facing work. Borrowed
from **Everlane's** radical transparency pricing, **Monzo** and **Oatly**.
Everlane built a brand by publishing what each item cost to make. Malaysian
families do not talk openly about education costs, and the private-university
category is defined by suspicion about hidden fees.

Publish the full number. Tuition, what PTPTN covers, what the scholarship covers,
accommodation, what is left. Line by line, per campus. This is not only a trend
borrow: overcoming IPTS bias and the "no hidden fee" objection is already a named
priority in the Cycle 3 planning matrix.

- Line: "Here is the full number. There is no second number."
  BM: "Ini angka penuh. Tiada angka kedua."
- Pairs directly with U1, the cost-after-PTPTN checker, and U2, the family pack.

**6. The 5 to 9 before the 9 to 5.** Serves Campaign C. Borrowed from the format
itself, which **Notion** and **Headspace** have both leaned on. An online MBA taken
while working full time is literally a 5-to-9. Brief TikTok One creators to film
their real study hour: laptop open after dinner, lecture playing while the kid
sleeps, 6am session before work. Seven of ten creator slots are free, postgrad is
the segment that performs there, and it satisfies the native-over-produced finding.

**7. Mamak Sessions.** Serves Campaign A. Borrowed from nothing foreign at all.
Malaysia already has a third place and it is the mamak. Put two counsellors at a
mamak near each campus on a Thursday night. No appointment, ask anything,
including what it costs.

This is the run club without the class problem. It costs the price of teh tarik,
reaches the B40 audience where they already are, and suits parents as much as
students, which matters because the parents pay. If the room backs only one
activation, this is the lowest risk and the most cultural truth.

### Mechanic borrows, small and cheap

- **Duolingo streak logic in the respond.io nurture layer.** The festive dead zone
  needs a reason for a warm lead to keep replying. A light weekly value drop with
  a visible countdown to the intake beats a silent gap and a cold call in January.
- **99 Speedmart as the positioning parallel for Campaign A.** 99 Speedmart won by
  being in every neighborhood, and "there is one near you" is its whole
  proposition. That is exactly the ten-campus story, and a sharper reference than
  any university.
- **AirAsia as the tone reference.** "Now Everyone Can Fly" turned a price position
  into a national identity. UNITAR's real position is the same democratization
  story. Do not copy the line, copy the confidence.

> **How to choose.** Study Hall and Mamak Sessions are cheap, culturally safe and
> can start in weeks. 12.12 needs only a date and a waiver. Campus Loop and Your
> Year Wrapped need real budget and a client champion. If the session picks two,
> take one cheap activation plus 12.12, and treat the run club as a two-campus
> pilot rather than a launch.

| C2 2026 plan, Mar to Jun | RM4,631,478, target approx 34,081 leads |
| Full RFP, six scopes | RM9.5M to RM11.5M |

The gap between the RM3.2M plan and the RM2.42M of summed monthly actuals needs
reconciling before it appears anywhere, since the plan figure is labeled net and
the monthlies may be media spend on a different basis. Do not present either as a
variance until that is checked.

Recommended shape for C1 2027: hold the envelope near the C1 2026 plan and change
the distribution, weighting November and December well above January and February.
That is the front-load argument, and it asks UNITAR for a sequencing change rather
than for more money, which is a much easier yes.

Channel mix should start from the C2 2026 pivot of SEM 30%, Meta lead generation
55%, TikTok lead generation 15%, with PMax staying off on the record of 0.96%
lead-to-application and RM10,513 per application. Within that, tilt toward Meta in
November and December and back toward Google in January.

Year-on-year spend already ran up 44%, RM9.1M against RM6.3M, with enrolment uplift
not proportional. Expect that to be raised. Answer it with the validation leak and
the front-load, not with more volume.
