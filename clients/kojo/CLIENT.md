# Kojo

Kojo sells materials procurement software to US trade contractors: field ordering, purchasing, invoice matching, warehouse, tool tracking and prefab. It is a sales-led company with no free trial and no product-led motion. Every inbound path ends in a demo, pricing or contact request. Site: [usekojo.com](https://www.usekojo.com/).

The client is called **Kojo** in deliverables. Jordan reversed the earlier "UseKojo" naming on 28 September 2026; usekojo.com is only the domain. This folder was merged from the old `clients/usekojo/` on 29 September 2026, so older documents and Drive folders still carry the UseKojo name.

Lawrence Quan (Kuota, the partner behind VoiceRun and SignWell) introduced us by email on 22 September 2026. Same referrer as SignWell (`clients/signwell/CLIENT.md`). His brief: short-term agency support to fully manage paid search and paid social while Kojo decides how to staff the role internally, plus a fresh view of the current campaigns.

## Company background (public sources, 2026-09-24)
- **History:** founded 2018 in San Francisco as Agora, renamed Kojo in 2022. About 150 staff. $83.6 million raised across four rounds.
- **Product:** seven modules. Field, Purchasing, Accounting, Warehouse, Prefab, Tool Tracking, Operations.
- **Integrations:** 13 ERP and accounting systems, Procore and Autodesk for project management, 10 distributor partners including Wesco, Graybar, Rexel and Border States.
- **Wesco** is both a distributor partner and an investor, having put $10M into a Series C extension in October 2025.
- **Claims they publish:** 75% less manual data entry, 4 hours saved per foreman per week, 3.5% saved on materials a year, 3% saved by catching invoice errors, 27% of invoices carry a discrepancy.
- **Traction:** $2 billion in materials orders processed a year (March 2024), 25,000 projects across 47 states, 15,000+ professionals on the platform, $30 million saved for customers.
- **Market:** construction procurement software is about $1.62B in 2026 heading to $2.60B by 2032. 331,067 US building equipment contractor establishments sit under NAICS 2382.

## Key numbers (from Alex, 24 September 2026)

- **Target spend:** $50,000 a month at most. The outgoing demand gen marketer was drafting the cut.
- **Cost per lead:** over $1,000, with no set target. Alex wants it "significantly" lower.
- **ACV:** about $30,000 a year mid-market, about $70,000 enterprise.
- **Sales cycle:** about 90 days on average; some deals close in 60 to 90.
- **Win rate:** "roughly 20%", stage not stated. Confirm whether this is demo to close or opportunity to close before using it.
- **Tracking accuracy:** the conversion point is right about 70 to 80% of the time.

## Audience
- **Geo:** United States only.
- **ICP:** electrical trade contractors first, mechanical second, with $25M+ annual revenue. Plumbing, HVAC and drywall are third tier: not excluded, not targeted.
- **Approach:** account-based. Kojo has its TAM and target accounts mapped. "Construction in general" is too broad.
- **Buying committee:** president or CEO signs at mid-market. Enterprise deals involve procurement, operations and field managers, with finance as a lower stakeholder. Project managers influence the decision but do not make it. The pre-call read was that the COO or VP Operations holds the budget, the purchasing manager is the daily user, and the field foreman decides whether it survives.
- **Planned test:** a project-manager campaign and landing page for a new PM-focused product.

## Funnel
- CTAs are all high intent: request a demo, request pricing, contact.
- Chili Piper books directly onto an account executive's calendar. Form fills without a booking go to the SDR team. No-shows are being routed back to SDRs.
- After the demo there is a three to five step evaluation.
- No lead scoring. Demo requests post to a Slack channel, with source attribution that works only some of the time.

## Competitors
- **Top three (per Alex):** Remarcable (vertical, started in plumbing and mechanical), Field Materials (horizontal; customers upload their own catalogs), Subbase (Alex is not up to speed; battle card promised).
- **Minor:** StructShare, acquired by Trimble.
- **Also in play:** Procore is the platform incumbent and an integration partner at once, and the spreadsheet is the real default.
- **Kojo's edge (per Alex):** pre-built distributor connections for better pricing, a stronger electrical catalog, and better UX. Field Materials and Remarcable market AI harder than Kojo, although Kojo has comparable features.
