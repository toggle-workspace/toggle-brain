# Kynare — KPI definition

## The operative KPI

**Cost per signed client, with cost per attended assessment as the leading indicator.**

The client's economics only make sense once the funnel is measured all the way through. A lead is not a customer, and on this account the gap between the two is large and currently unmeasured.

## The four-stage funnel we must report

| Stage | Source | Who owns the number |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Leads submitted | Meta and TikTok lead forms, WhatsApp conversations started | Toggle, from the platforms |
| 2. Assessments booked | Client's booking system after the team follows up | Client |
| 3. Assessments attended (walk-ins) | Client | Client |
| 4. Packages signed | Client | Client |

Toggle can only see stage 1. **Stages 2 to 4 must be chased from the client every reporting cycle.** Without them the RM180 to RM250 target CAC cannot be verified, and no budget decision on this account is defensible.

## What the KPI is NOT

- **Not cost per lead.** CPL is the tactical metric the media buyer optimizes against day to day. It is not the headline, and it must never be reported as if it were the CAC.
- **Not lead volume.** The clinic has 130 client-hours a week and three practitioners. Leads the team cannot see are worthless.
- **Not blended across locations without a note.** Both clinics sit in one ad set, so the platform cannot split them. The preferred-location field on the form is the only attribution available. Say so in the report rather than implying the split is measured.

## Why the show-up rate is the number that matters most

The business model is education-first: the client closes reliably once someone walks in. That makes the walk-in, not the lead, the real purchase moment.

Worked example of why this cannot be assumed:

- 10 leads a week, 50% show rate, 80% close rate = **4 sign-ups a week**
- 10 leads a week, 80% show rate, 80% close rate = **6.4 sign-ups a week**

Those two scenarios need different budgets to hit the same target. Nobody has this number as of 2026-08-12. Getting it is the first reporting priority.

## Reference points for judging performance

- Holding **20 active clients** on a six-week cycle needs roughly **3.3 sign-ups a week** in steady state.
- Ten sign-ups a week builds toward roughly **60 active clients**, near the practical ceiling.
- At RM3,000 to RM4,500 a month and RM180 to RM250 CAC, the plan buys 12 to 25 sign-ups a month, which is 2.8 to 5.8 a week. That is correctly sized for the 20-active target and close to its floor.
- Blended revenue per signed client is **RM3,202** including the RM250 sign-up fee (the client's own RM2,950 figure excludes it).

## Segments to report from week one

- **By campaign** (Meta lead form, Meta WhatsApp, TikTok lead form)
- **By age band.** The client base skews older while targeting starts at 18. Cheap leads from the young end flatter CPL and damage CAC. Report the bands so the cut is made on evidence.
- **By language** (English, Bahasa Malaysia)
- **By preferred location** (PJ Section 13, Mont Kiara), from the form field

## Anti-goals (binding)

- Reporting cost per lead as the headline result.
- Presenting a location split as measured when it is self-reported on the form.
- Scaling budget on lead volume before the show-up rate is known.
- Adding any location outside PJ and Mont Kiara. Every lead has to physically walk into one of the two clinics.
