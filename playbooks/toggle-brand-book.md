## PAGE — The Three Pillars: In Practice

The mission on the cover is the headline. These three pillars are the operating system. Every client engagement, every piece of creative, every report format should be traceable back to at least one of them. If you cannot trace it back, you should not be shipping it.

**Pillar 1: Built to Grow the Whole Business**

Most agencies optimise for the metric that looks good in the monthly deck. Toggle builds full-funnel systems — paid acquisition, creative, lifecycle, and reporting — because a business that only improves top-of-funnel is not actually growing. A lead that never converts is a cost, not a result. We work across ads, design, CRM, and analytics simultaneously because partial systems produce partial growth.

*What this means in practice:* When we take on a new client, we audit all four layers before recommending anything. If we only have mandate over one layer, we say so and flag the ceiling that creates.

**Pillar 2: One Growth System Across Every Channel**

When Meta, Google, TikTok, email, and SEO each have separate strategies, they compete for budget, attribution credit, and attention. When they share a single strategy — common messaging, a shared conversion framework, unified reporting — they compound. A brand that audiences see consistently across three channels requires 4× fewer touchpoints to convert than a brand they see inconsistently across six.

*What this means in practice:* We present channel performance inside a single reporting framework. We don't let a client say "Google is underperforming" until we've accounted for the assist traffic that Google sends to Meta.

**Pillar 3: Senior-Led, In-House Execution**

The standard agency model hands strategy to a senior team member and execution to a junior. The client meets the senior in the pitch and the junior in every meeting after that. Toggle inverts this. The people who designed your strategy are the people who build the ads, write the briefs, pull the reports, and join the calls. Fewer handoffs. Fewer translation errors. Faster decisions.

*What this means in practice:* Client-facing team members at Toggle have at least three years of hands-on execution experience in their domain, not just strategy or account management.

## PAGE — Logo: Philosophy

The Toggle wordmark is a statement of position. Lowercase. Direct. The toggle-switch mark is not decorative — it literalises the brand's core idea: flipping the switch from the old way (agency, passive, junior-led) to the new way (partner, active, senior-led). The mark is precise and geometric. It does not flex. Do not redraw it, recreate it from a stock icon, or substitute it with a different symbol.

> **VISUAL:** Side-by-side: correct wordmark (white on navy) and an incorrect example (wordmark in a rounded badge with drop-shadow). Large red X on the incorrect version. Caption: "The toggle mark is a dedicated asset — not a generic switch icon from any icon library."

## PAGE — Logo: Mark Construction

> **VISUAL:** The toggle-switch mark drawn on an 8×8 keyline grid, with construction measurements annotated in cobalt lines. The mark is a rounded rectangle "track" (6 grid units wide × 2 units tall, corner radius = 1 unit) with a filled circular "thumb" (1.6 units diameter) positioned at the right end of the track. Stroke weight on the track outline: 0.25 units.
>
> Annotations:
> - Track width: 6u  / Track height: 2u  / Corner radius: 1u
> - Thumb diameter: 1.6u  / Thumb position: thumb-centre at 4.8u along the track
> - Stroke weight (track outline): 0.25u
> - All values scale proportionally with the mark — `u` = the unit grid square, sized to match the wordmark's lowercase "t" height.

**The geometry has meaning.** The thumb is positioned at the right ("on") end of the track, not centred. The switch is flipped. Toggle's brand premise — moving the client from the old way to the new way — is encoded into the mark itself. Never draw the mark with the thumb on the left.

**Reproduction rules:**
- Always work from the master SVG. Do not redraw the mark from the description above for production work.
- Minimum size for the mark in isolation: 16×16px digital / 5mm print.
- Mark colour is always cobalt (#2F6BFF) on dark surfaces; on a single-colour run (one-ink print), use the brand colour appropriate to that surface — never red, never yellow.

> **VISUAL — DO:** Mark drawn from the SVG at intended scale, cobalt on navy.
>
> **VISUAL — DON'T:** Mark with the thumb centred in the track, or thumb on the left — the brand point is lost the moment the switch reads as "off."

## PAGE — Colour: Philosophy

Colour is Toggle's most legible signal. The deep navy is not "professional" or "trustworthy" by convention — it is the colour of systems built at night, of dashboards that tell the truth, of partners who work while clients sleep. The cobalt is the action colour: it lives on every CTA, every interactive element, every arrow that says "move forward." The cyan is data — the colour of metrics, charts, and the moment a number confirms what you suspected.

One accent. One action colour. One data colour. One neutral scale. Everything else is surface and text. This is enough. Brands with six accent colours are brands that can't make decisions.

## PAGE — Typography: Philosophy

Toggle uses one family. Inter. The decision is deliberate: Inter was designed for screens, optimised for dashboard readability, and comes in weights from Thin to ExtraBold. It has a large x-height that makes it legible at 12px on a low-density display and beautiful at 72px in a pitch deck. A single-family system removes the decision fatigue of pairing, eliminates font-loading overhead, and keeps every surface — from a client's monthly report to the toggle.solutions homepage — reading like one coherent entity.

InterDisplay is used for display sizes (32px and above). Inter is used for everything below. Both are the same design family. The split is about rendering optimisation, not aesthetics.

## PAGE — Typography: Alignment & Case Rules

**Alignment:**
- Body copy: Left-aligned. Always. No centred paragraphs.
- Display headings: Left-aligned default. Centred is acceptable only on full-bleed hero sections where the text is the sole content.
- Numbers in tables: Right-aligned.
- Navigation items: Centre-aligned in horizontal navs, left-aligned in vertical navs.
- Arabic / RTL: Mirror to right-aligned text, left-aligned numbers. (Currently no active RTL requirement — document here when it arises.)

**Case:**
- Headlines: Title Case for display headings. Sentence case for h2 and below.
- Labels / overlines: ALL CAPS, tracked at +80 letter-spacing (approx 0.08em).
- Body: Sentence case. Always.
- CTAs (buttons): Sentence case. Not ALL CAPS. Not Title Case.

> **VISUAL — DO:** Button reading "Get a free consultation"
>
> **VISUAL — DON'T:** Button reading "GET A FREE CONSULTATION" — all-caps in a button makes the text 30% harder to parse and removes warmth.

## PAGE — Motion: Principles

Motion at Toggle communicates state change, not personality. Every animated element exists to tell the user something changed — a form submitted, a filter applied, a panel expanded. Motion that exists only for aesthetic interest is removed.

**Three motion principles:**

1. **Purposeful** — If you can remove the animation and nothing is lost in comprehension, remove it.
2. **Fast** — Default durations are shorter than you think. Most transitions complete in 150–250ms. Longer animations signal loading or processing — not style.
3. **Directional** — Elements enter from a meaningful direction (dropdowns fall down, drawers slide from the right) rather than scaling from centre.

---

## PAGE — Motion: Timing & Easing Tokens

| Token | Duration | Easing | Use |
|-------|----------|--------|-----|
| motion-instant | 0ms | — | State changes with no visual delay (toggle switch flip) |
| motion-fast | 150ms | ease-out | Hover states, focus rings, colour transitions |
| motion-default | 250ms | ease-in-out | Panel expand/collapse, tab switches |
| motion-slow | 400ms | ease-in-out | Page-level transitions, modal open |
| motion-entrance | 300ms | cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) | Elements entering the viewport |

**Easing rationale:**
- `ease-out` for fast hover states — starts quick, ends gentle, feels responsive.
- `ease-in-out` for default transitions — symmetrical, reads as deliberate.
- `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for entrances — a fast initial burst that settles smoothly, matching the "operator energy" of the brand.

## PAGE — Tone of Voice: Philosophy

Toggle's voice is the sound of an operator, not a presenter. An operator has already done the thinking. When an operator speaks, it is to communicate a decision, a result, or a next step — not to demonstrate expertise or build rapport through volume of words. Every line of Toggle copy should feel like it was written by someone who is already working on your growth, not trying to win a pitch.

## PAGE — Tone of Voice: Four Axes

Following the Nielsen Norman Group framework, Toggle's voice is defined on four axes:

| Axis | Toggle Position | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Funny ↔ Serious | 70% Serious | We can be dry. We are not comedic. Results are not funny. |
| Formal ↔ Casual | 40% Casual | Accessible, direct, professional. Not corporate. Not bro. |
| Respectful ↔ Irreverent | 60% Respectful | The struck-through device is our one major irreverent move. Use it sparingly. |
| Enthusiastic ↔ Matter-of-fact | 65% Matter-of-fact | We state what happened and what it means. Exclamation marks are earned. |

## PAGE — Tone of Voice: Principles

**1. Outcome before process.**
Lead with the result. Follow with how it was achieved. Never the reverse.

*Wrong:* "We used a combination of Meta instant forms and Google Performance Max campaigns targeting high-intent audiences to generate qualified leads."
*Right:* "We drove 597 leads in 30 days at RM43 CPL. Here's the setup."

**2. Specificity is credibility.**
Vague claims are indistinguishable from noise. Numbers, timeframes, and named tactics are credible. "Significant growth" is not.

*Wrong:* "We helped UNITAR achieve significant improvements in their digital marketing performance."
*Right:* "UNITAR's cost-per-lead dropped 37% in the first 60 days. Spend was up 126%. Growth is not free — but it should be efficient."

**3. Assume sophistication.**
The client has read the same playbooks you have. Don't explain what a conversion funnel is. Do explain what Toggle's specific version achieves that the standard version doesn't.

**4. No filler confidence.**
Phrases like "best-in-class," "holistic approach," "seamless integration," and "synergistic solutions" are removed on sight. If a phrase cannot be disproved, it has no information content.

**5. The brand speaks first in the headline.**
Headlines carry the argument. If the body copy disappeared, the headline should still communicate something true and specific. "Digital marketing services" is not a headline. "37% lower CPL in 60 days" is.

## PAGE — Grammar & Style: Rules

**Sentence case everywhere.** Headlines, CTAs, navigation items, and card headings are all sentence case. Title Case is used only for proper nouns and the brand name.

- Correct: "Full-stack digital solutions"
- Incorrect: "Full-Stack Digital Solutions"

**Oxford comma: Yes.** "Ads, creative, and reporting" — not "Ads, creative and reporting."

**Numbers:**
- Spell out one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above.
- Exception: always use numerals for metrics, percentages, prices, and statistics. "37% lower CPL" not "thirty-seven percent lower CPL."
- Currency: RM for Malaysian Ringgit. Always before the number. RM43, not MYR 43.00.
- Thousands: use comma separator. RM1,000 not RM1000.

**Dates:**
- Day Month Year, no ordinal suffixes. "23 May 2026" not "23rd May, 2026."
- Month abbreviations: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec.

**Contractions:** Allowed and encouraged in conversational contexts (case study intros, social copy, CTAs). Avoid in formal reports and proposals.

**Em dashes (—):** Use sparingly for sentence emphasis. Space on both sides.

**Ellipses (...):** Never in professional copy. Either end the sentence or don't.

**Exclamation marks:** One per document, maximum. Earned.

**Bold:** For in-paragraph emphasis only. One or two words. Not a sentence. Not a strategy.

**Italics:** For titles of external works (publications, films) and for light rhetorical emphasis. Not a substitute for structural emphasis.

## PAGE — Accessibility: Standards

Toggle targets **WCAG 2.2 Level AA** across every digital surface we ship — ours and our clients'. Accessibility is a craft standard, not a legal one. We hit it because copy that's clear for a screen-reader user is clear for everyone, and contrast that holds on a cracked phone in direct sunlight is contrast that holds in a conference room.

**Contrast requirements:**
- Normal text (below 18pt, below 14pt Bold): minimum 4.5:1
- Large text (18pt+ or 14pt+ Bold): minimum 3:1
- Non-text elements (icons, borders, charts): minimum 3:1

**Images and icons:**
- All informational images have alt text
- Decorative images have empty alt text (alt="")
- Icons used alone (without visible label) have aria-label

**Motion and animation:**
- All animations respect `prefers-reduced-motion` media query
- When reduced-motion is set, transitions default to opacity-only, 150ms or instant

## PAGE — The Crossed-Out Device: What It Is

The crossed-out device is Toggle's most distinctive brand mark after the logo. It appears once — prominently — on the homepage hero. That restraint is what gives it power. If it appeared on every page, it would be a style trick. Used once, it is a position statement.

The device works because it does three things simultaneously:
1. It names the category Toggle rejects (marketing agency)
2. It replaces that name with the truer one (digital growth partner)
3. It signals that Toggle has already thought about the distinction, so the prospect doesn't have to

The visual mechanic — the struck-out line — does the rhetorical work. It shows the thinking process. It reads as honest, not polished. That is intentional.

---

## PAGE — The Crossed-Out Device: When to Use It

**Use the device:**
- In hero sections where Toggle is introducing itself for the first time
- In pitch decks, on the cover or the first positioning slide
- In campaign ads where the audience is likely to be "shopping agencies" (i.e., actively comparing options)
- In brand awareness content where the positioning distinction needs to be made explicit

**Do not use the device:**
- Repeatedly within the same document or page
- For any claim that isn't genuinely a position Toggle rejects (it must be honest)
- In reporting documents or operational communications — the device is marketing, not operations
- In a context where the struck-out text could be misread as an error or a legal retraction

## PAGE — Localisation: Philosophy

Toggle is a Malaysian company that writes mostly in English. Most of our clients run their internal language in English. Their customers don't. A campaign that ships only in English in Malaysia leaves at least 30% of the addressable audience reading the second-best version of the message.

Bahasa Malaysia is not a translation pass we run at the end of a sprint. It is a peer language. We write the message in the language it will live in, then we check the English. Transcreation, not translation. Mandarin (SEA-simplified) is the third language in our active rotation — used for retail clients with Chinese-Malaysian audiences and for any campaign that crosses into Singapore.

When the message is wrong in BM, no amount of media spend fixes it. When it is right, the CPL gap closes in the first 14 days.

---

## PAGE — Localisation: Translation vs Transcreation

Translation moves words from one language to another. Transcreation moves meaning. Toggle does transcreation. Direct-translated copy is a tell — the audience can read the original language behind it.

**Worked example — the positioning device:**

| Mode | EN | BM |
|------|-----|------|
| Direct translation | ~~Your Marketing Agency~~ → Your Digital Growth Partner | ~~Agensi Pemasaran Anda~~ → Rakan Pertumbuhan Digital Anda |
| Transcreation | ~~Your Marketing Agency~~ → Your Digital Growth Partner | ~~Sekadar Agensi~~ → Sistem Pertumbuhan Anda |

The direct version is grammatical but flat — it reads like a brochure that got translated by an intern. The transcreated version keeps the rhetorical move (rejecting the lazy expectation) and sounds like it was written by someone who speaks BM as their first language. "Sekadar" carries a dismissive shrug that "Pemasaran Anda" doesn't.

**Rule:** If the BM line reads like a literal swap of the EN line, it is wrong. Send it back.

## PAGE — Localisation: When to Use BM

Toggle does not run every campaign in every language. Pick deliberately.

**Default to BM when:**
- The client's customer base is majority Malay-speaking (consumer retail, F&B, education in non-urban segments)
- The campaign creative will appear in print or out-of-home in Malaysia outside KL / Selangor
- A specific channel (e.g., TikTok in MY) skews to BM-first audiences

**Default to English when:**
- The client is B2B and the buying centre operates in English
- The campaign is targeting KL / Selangor / Penang urban segments with high English-medium consumption
- The brand has an English-only brand identity by deliberate choice

**Default to both when:**
- The audience reads code-switched copy as natural (most Malaysian digital audiences under 40 do — a single ad with EN headline and BM CTA often outperforms a single-language version)

**Where to ask:** If you cannot decide, default to running both as a test. Don't guess.

## PAGE — Resources: Glossary

**Blended CPL** — Cost per lead calculated across all channels combined. Useful for reporting total acquisition efficiency. Different from channel-specific CPL.

**Compound growth** — Growth where each period's results build on the previous period because the system retains what it learned (audience data, creative learnings, keyword refinement). As opposed to campaign-by-campaign growth that resets.

**Crossed-out device** — Toggle's positioning visual: a struck-through wrong name replaced by the right name. Documents: "Your ~~Marketing Agency~~ Digital Growth Partner." Use once per surface.

**CPL** — Cost per lead. Calculated as spend ÷ leads generated. Lower is better, holding lead quality constant.

**Full-funnel** — A strategy that covers all stages of the customer journey: awareness, consideration, conversion, and retention. As opposed to top-of-funnel-only (brand awareness) or bottom-of-funnel-only (performance).

**Growth system** — Toggle's preferred term for its service offer. A system implies ongoing, compounding operation as opposed to a campaign, which implies a time-boxed event.

**Instant forms** — Meta's on-platform lead forms (as opposed to driving traffic to an external website landing page). Generally lower CPL because they remove the friction of a website visit.

**ROAS** — Return on ad spend. Revenue generated ÷ ad spend. A ROAS of 3× means RM3 revenue for every RM1 spent.

**Senior-led** — Toggle's team structure claim: the person who designed the strategy is the person executing it. No strategy-to-junior handoff.

**SPM** — In the UNITAR context: Sijil Pelajaran Malaysia — a specific educational programme segment with a distinct search audience and campaign structure.

**Surface** — A designed context where the brand appears. Examples: homepage, email, pitch deck, paid social ad, printed case study. Each surface has slightly different type size, colour usage, and tone requirements, all documented in this manual.

**Tokens** — Named design values (colours, sizes, spacing) stored in `tokens.json` and consumed by code. Tokens ensure the design system and the codebase stay in sync.

**WCAG** — Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. The international standard for digital accessibility. Toggle targets WCAG 2.2 Level AA on all client-facing digital surfaces.
