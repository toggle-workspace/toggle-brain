# Proposal IP Protection Pack — Toggle Solutions

**Purpose:** stop a prospect from taking a Toggle proposal, thanking us politely, and running the idea in-house or with a cheaper agency.
**Jurisdiction:** Malaysia. Drafted against the Copyright Act 1987, Contracts Act 1950, Stamp Act 1949 and Malaysian common law on breach of confidence.
**Status:** template. Have a Malaysian-qualified lawyer review Part B once before it becomes your standard form. After that it is reusable without further advice.
**Owner:** Toggle Solutions · **Version:** 1.0 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-05

---

## 0. Read this first — the one thing that actually matters

Malaysian copyright does **not** protect ideas. Section 7(2A) of the Copyright Act 1987 says copyright protection "shall not extend to any idea, procedure, method of operation or mathematical concept as such."

So if a client reads our deck and their in-house team runs *"45 × 2-minute micro-series tied to a salted-coffee SKU"* — the **idea** is not copyright-infringing. Only our **expression** of it (the deck layout, the copy, the storyboards, the artwork) is.

That gap is closed by **contract**, not by copyright. The protection stack, in order of how well it actually works:

| Layer | Mechanism | Strength | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Commercial lock** — paid discovery, staged disclosure, deposit | Strongest. No lawyer needed. | Low |
| 2 | **Signed agreement** (Part B) — contractual non-use | Strong. Enforceable. | Medium |
| 1 | **Notice on the deck** (Part A) — legend + copyright line | Weak alone. Deterrent + evidence. | Zero |

**Never rely on Layer 1 alone.** A confidentiality legend on a PDF is a deterrent and useful evidence of intent, but a client who never signed anything can argue no contract was formed. Use all three layers on any proposal worth more than ~RM20,000.

### Which document to use when

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Cold inbound, small scope (< RM20k), generic scope-of-work quote | Part A legend only |
| Named client, real strategy in the deck, RM20k–RM100k | Part A + Part B (signed before deck is sent) |
| Big pitch, RM100k+, or a competitive RFP against other agencies | Part A + Part B + Part C paid discovery |
| Client refuses to sign anything | Part C only — withhold the "how", pitch the "what" and "why", charge for the rest |
| Enterprise client insists on *their* NDA | Accept it, but bolt on the Part B clauses 4, 6 and 12 as an addendum |

## PART A — Proposal Legend (drop into every proposal and deck)

Two placements. Both. Every time.

### A.1 — Cover page block (full version)

> **CONFIDENTIAL — PREPARED EXCLUSIVELY FOR [CLIENT NAME]**
>
> This document and everything in it — including the strategy, campaign concepts, creative angles, content formats, funnel structures, offer construction, media plans, targeting logic, naming and measurement frameworks — is the confidential property of **Toggle Solutions Sdn Bhd** and is protected under the Copyright Act 1987 and the law of confidence.
>
> It is supplied for the sole purpose of allowing [CLIENT NAME] to evaluate whether to appoint Toggle Solutions. It is **not** a licence to use, implement, adapt or disclose any part of it.
>
> No part of this document may be executed, implemented or replicated — by [CLIENT NAME], by its employees or in-house team, or by any third party agency, contractor or freelancer — in whole, in part, or in materially similar form, without a signed engagement with Toggle Solutions and payment of the applicable fees.
>
> By continuing to read, circulate or retain this document, the recipient accepts these terms.
>
> © 2026 Toggle Solutions Sdn Bhd. All rights reserved. Document ref: **[REF]** · Issued to: **[RECIPIENT NAME, EMAIL]** · Issued on: **[DATE]**

### A.2 — Footer on every single page (short version)

> Confidential — © 2026 Toggle Solutions Sdn Bhd. Evaluation use only. Not for implementation without a signed engagement. Ref [REF] · [RECIPIENT NAME] · [DATE]

**Non-negotiable rules for the footer:**
- The recipient's name goes in the footer of *their* copy only. Every recipient gets a uniquely stamped PDF. If the deck leaks, the footer tells you who leaked it.
- Never send an editable file. PDF only, with the legend flattened into the page.
- Keep a copy of the exact file you sent, in the folder for that client, with the send date.

**11.3** Payment of the Agreed Sum is without prejudice to Toggle's right to injunctive relief under clause 10, and to its right to elect instead to claim damages for its actual loss or an account of the Recipient's profits.

> **How to set the Agreed Sum.** Under *Cubic Electronics Sdn Bhd v Mars Telecommunications Sdn Bhd* the Federal Court held that a stipulated sum is recoverable only so far as it is **reasonable compensation** — an inflated number invites the court to cut it down. Peg it to something defensible, not to a scary round figure:
> - **Retainer work:** 3 months of the quoted monthly retainer.
> - **Project work:** 50% of the total quoted project fee.
> - **Big pitch:** the quoted fee, plus a documented figure for the pitch cost.
>
> Write the actual number into 11.1 for every deal. Never leave it blank, and never use the same number on every contract — a one-size-fits-all figure is exactly what looks unreasonable in court.

## PART C — Commercial locks (the layer that actually works)

Litigation is slow and expensive. These mechanisms cost nothing and prevent the problem instead of remedying it. Use them on every deal above RM20k.

### C.1 Staged disclosure — sell the *what* and *why*, charge for the *how*

The existing `templates/proposals/proposal.md` already says the Approach section carries "the strategic shape of how we'd solve it — not the tactical detail." Enforce that.

| Goes in the free proposal | Held back until signed |
|---|---|
| The commercial problem, reframed | The specific hooks, scripts, angles |
| The strategic direction ("short-form series tied to SKU velocity") | The 45-episode content matrix |
| Which channels, and why | Targeting stacks, audience definitions, bid structures |
| Named deliverables and quantities | The actual creative, storyboards, copy |
| Case evidence and benchmark numbers | The playbook that produced those numbers |
| Timeline shape and pricing | Week-by-week execution schedule |

A client can steal a direction. A direction without the execution detail is worth far less, and they usually come back for the detail.

### C.2 Paid discovery / strategy sprint, credited on signature

The strongest protection available. Restructure the free proposal into:

> **Strategy Sprint — RM [5,000–15,000]**
> 2 weeks. Deep audit, competitive teardown, full strategy document, campaign architecture, measurement framework.
> **100% credited against your first three months of retainer if you appoint us within 30 days.**

Effects, all of them good:
- Free-riders self-select out at the first email. You stop writing decks for tyre-kickers.
- If they take the strategy and run, you were paid for the strategy.
- Clause 6.2 keeps the copyright with Toggle even though they paid — so paying for the sprint does not buy the right to implement it.
- Because they pay, the engagement is properly commissioned work and the confidentiality obligation is far easier to prove.

### C.3 Deposit before creative

Never produce named creative, scripts or storyboards before a signed SOW and a deposit clears. Concepts presented as one-line descriptions in a pitch; executed creative only after money moves.

### C.4 Pitch fees for competitive RFPs

If a client is running you against three other agencies and wants full creative, ask for a pitch fee (RM3,000–10,000 is normal in the Malaysian market for a full creative pitch). If they refuse, present strategy only and hold all creative. A client unwilling to pay anything for four agencies' work is telling you exactly what they intend to do with it.

### C.5 Validity and expiry

Every proposal carries: *"This proposal and the pricing in it are valid for 30 days from the date of issue. After that date, the Concepts remain confidential and subject to the Agreement, but the pricing lapses."* This kills the "we'll revisit next year" slow-walk that ends in an in-house build.

## PART D — Evidence hygiene (do this on every proposal, takes 5 minutes)

If it ever goes to a letter of demand, the case is won or lost on whether you can prove **what** you disclosed, **when**, and **to whom**. Build the file as you go.

1. **Unique watermark per recipient.** Recipient's name and email in the footer of their copy. Different ref number per recipient. If it appears in a competitor's hands, you know the route.
2. **PDF only, flattened, never editable.** No PPTX, no Google Slides with edit or comment rights. View-only links with download disabled where possible.
3. **Send by email, not by WhatsApp.** Email is a timestamped, self-authenticating trail. Attach the PDF rather than linking, so the file itself sits in the thread. Keep the sent copy.
4. **Keep the development trail.** Drafts, briefs, research notes, Notion and Slack timestamps, meeting notes. This is what proves independent authorship and defeats "we'd already thought of that."
5. **Version and date every file.** `Client-Proposal-v3-2026-09-05.pdf`. Store in `toggle-brain/clients/<client>/` alongside the send record.
6. **Voluntary notification with MyIPO.** For high-value or signature concepts, file a voluntary notification of copyright under section 26A of the Copyright Act 1987 (Copyright (Voluntary Notification) Regulations 2012). It is cheap, and the certificate is **prima facie evidence** of ownership and of the date — which removes the single hardest thing to prove in a copyright claim. Worth doing for anything above RM50k.
7. **A statutory declaration of authorship** for the crown-jewel concepts. A commissioner for oaths costs a few ringgit; the declaration is admissible and fixes the date.
8. **Monitor after the pitch.** Set a calendar reminder 60 and 120 days after any lost or silent pitch. Check the client's socials, and check the **Meta Ad Library** and **TikTok Creative Center** — both are public, searchable by advertiser, and archive creative with dates. This is the cheapest infringement-detection tool that exists and it produces court-ready screenshots.

## PART E — If it happens: the enforcement ladder

**Step 1 — Preserve evidence, before you say a word.**
Screenshot everything with visible dates. Pull the ads from Meta Ad Library (it shows launch dates). Archive the pages via web.archive.org. Download the videos. Do this first — content disappears the moment you complain.

**Step 2 — Build the comparison.**
A side-by-side table: our concept, their execution, date we disclosed, date they launched. Specificity wins. Ten points of similarity is a case; "it feels like ours" is not.

**Step 3 — Decide what you actually want.**
Usually it is payment and a credit, not a fight. Most of these resolve commercially once the client realises you have a signed agreement, a dated deck and screenshots.

**Step 4 — Letter of demand.**
From a solicitor, on a firm's letterhead. Not from you. Plead all available causes:
- **Breach of contract** — clauses 4.1 and 6 of Part B.
- **Breach of confidence** — the equitable claim, which reaches the *idea* where copyright cannot. Malaysian courts apply the three-part test in *Coco v A N Clark (Engineers) Ltd* [1969] RPC 41: (i) the information had the necessary quality of confidence; (ii) it was imparted in circumstances importing an obligation of confidence; (iii) there was unauthorised use to the discloser's detriment. **This is your primary weapon for stolen ideas.**
- **Copyright infringement** — sections 36 and 37 of the Copyright Act 1987, where they have copied the *expression* (decks, scripts, artwork, copy). Remedies include damages, injunction, account of profits, delivery up and statutory damages.
- **Unjust enrichment** — where they took the benefit without paying.
- **Liquidated damages** — clause 11.

**Step 5 — Injunction, if they are still running the campaign.**
An interlocutory injunction under Order 29 of the Rules of Court 2012. The Malaysian test is set out in *Keet Gerald Francis Noel John v Mohd Noor bin Abdullah* [1995] 1 MLJ 193 (CA): a bona fide serious issue to be tried, where the balance of justice lies, and the adequacy of damages. Move fast — delay is fatal to an injunction application.

**Step 6 — Limitation.**
Six years from the breach, under the Limitation Act 1953. But in practice, act within weeks, not years.

> **Honest note on cost.** A contested IP suit in the Malaysian High Court runs into six figures and takes years. That is precisely why Part C matters more than Part B, and Part B matters more than Part A. The agreement's real value is that it makes the letter of demand credible enough that you never have to file.

## PART F — Malaysian law limits you need to know

These are the places where an English or American template will get you into trouble. Read them before editing Part B.

**1. Copyright does not protect ideas — s.7(2A), Copyright Act 1987.**
Your protection for the *idea* is contract and breach of confidence. That is why clause 1.1 defines "Concepts" to include things "whether or not capable of protection under the Copyright Act 1987." Do not delete that phrase.

**2. Restraint of trade is void — s.28, Contracts Act 1950.**
This is the big one, and it is *not* the English position. Malaysia has no "reasonableness" saving test: section 28 renders every agreement restraining a person from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business void, subject only to narrow statutory exceptions. Restraints operating during the currency of a contract have been upheld (*Polygram Records Sdn Bhd v The Search* [1994] 3 MLJ 127), but post-contractual restraints are routinely struck down.

Practical consequences:
- **Never** put a non-compete on a client. It will be void and it makes the whole document look aggressive.
- Clause 4.1 is drafted as a **non-use of confidential information** obligation, not a restraint on the client's trade. That is why clause 4.3 exists — it records that the client remains free to trade and to hire any other agency, just not to use *our* confidential material. Keep 4.3.
- Clause 5 (non-circumvention) is the most vulnerable provision in the document. Keep it short, keep it to twelve months, keep it limited to parties Toggle actually introduced, and keep clause 5.3 so a court can sever it without taking clauses 3, 4 and 6 down with it.

**3. Liquidated damages must be reasonable — s.75, Contracts Act 1950.**
Following *Cubic Electronics Sdn Bhd v Mars Telecommunications Sdn Bhd* [2019] 2 CLJ 723 (FC), the innocent party must prove the breach and that the stipulated sum is reasonable compensation. An arbitrary or punitive figure gets reduced. See the guidance box under clause 11.

**4. Commissioned work vests in the client by default — s.26(2), Copyright Act 1987.**
Under Malaysian law, where a work is commissioned, copyright is **deemed transferred to the person who commissioned it**, unless the agreement excludes or limits that transfer. This is the opposite of what most people assume, and it is the single most dangerous default in Malaysian copyright law for an agency. Clause 6.2 exists specifically to switch it off. It matters most the moment a client pays you anything — a pitch fee, a discovery fee — because that is when the work starts to look commissioned. **Never remove clause 6.2, and make sure the equivalent clause appears in every Toggle SOW and retainer, not just this one.**

**5. Assignments must be in writing — s.27(3), Copyright Act 1987.**
Copyright never passes by handshake, invoice or conduct. When you do hand IP over on final payment, do it by an express written assignment.

**6. An unstamped agreement is inadmissible — s.52, Stamp Act 1949.**
An instrument that is not duly stamped cannot be received in evidence. A general agreement attracts nominal duty (historically RM10 per copy under Item 4 of the First Schedule); stamp within 30 days of execution to avoid penalties. Malaysia is transitioning to a self-assessment stamp duty regime, so confirm the current rate and procedure on the LHDN portal before filing. **An NDA you never stamped is an NDA you cannot use.** Build stamping into the signing checklist.

**7. Electronic signatures are valid — Electronic Commerce Act 2006.**
DocuSign, Adobe Sign or an equivalent is fine for this agreement. Keep the audit certificate with the signed PDF.

**8. Execution formalities — s.66, Companies Act 2016.**
A company executes by two authorised officers (one of whom must be a director), or by a sole director in the presence of a witness. Check the client's signatory is actually authorised — a signature from a marketing manager with no authority is a hole in your case. Ask for a director, or for board authorisation.

**9. Personal Data Protection Act 2010.**
Amended with effect through 2025 (including mandatory breach notification and data protection officer requirements). Relevant whenever a pitch involves client customer data. Clause 12 covers the basics; a data-sharing pitch needs more than clause 12.

## APPENDIX — Sending the NDA without killing the deal

The commonest objection is not legal, it is social: asking for an NDA feels like accusing the client of theft. Frame it as process, not suspicion.

**Email template:**

> Subject: Before I send the [CLIENT] proposal — one quick form
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Really enjoyed the conversation on [DATE]. I'm putting the strategy together now and should have it with you by [DATE].
>
> One housekeeping item first. Because the deck will contain our full campaign architecture and the specific creative angles — rather than a generic capability overview — we send it under a standard two-way confidentiality note. It's the same form we use with every client and it protects your side too: anything you've shared with us about [their numbers / roadmap / launch timing] is covered by the same terms.
>
> It's [two] pages, e-signature, takes a minute. Link here: [LINK]
>
> Once it's back I'll send the full proposal straight over.
>
> Best,
> [NAME]

**Handling pushback:**

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We don't sign NDAs at proposal stage." | "Understood — that's common. In that case I'll send the strategic direction and the commercials, and we'll cover the executional detail in our first working session once we're engaged. Happy either way." *(Then use Part C.1 and hold the detail.)* |
| "Just send it, we're trustworthy." | "Of course — this isn't about trust, it's that our own client agreements require it on anything with named creative in it." |
| "Send it to our legal team." | Good sign. Expect redlines on clauses 5 and 11. Concede clause 5 before you concede clause 4 or 6. |
| "We'll use our own NDA." | Fine. Read it for a mutual non-use clause and an IP clause. If it only covers confidentiality and not **non-implementation**, bolt on clauses 4, 6 and 12 as a one-page addendum. |

**Never concede:** clause 4 (non-use), clause 6.1 and 6.2 (IP ownership and s.26(2) exclusion).
**Tradeable:** clause 5 (non-circumvention), clause 11 (liquidated damages), the 12-month period in 4.1 (9 months is still useful), the 3-year term in 9.1.
